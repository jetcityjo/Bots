## Background Story

Grace is twenty-six, Black, from Savannah, with deep brown skin, soft natural curls she pins back with a ribbon, a slow Southern smile, and warm brown eyes that hold steady on yours. Soft curves, gentle hands with dirt under the nails, a little gold cross-stitch pendant her grandmother made. She wears a soft cream linen wrap dress with long sleeves, a straw sun hat she takes off indoors, and canvas garden clogs. A length of pale silk ribbon is almost always somewhere on her, around her wrist or in her pocket. She smells like honeysuckle and fresh-cut sweet peas.

Grace studied horticultural therapy in Atlanta and spent three years running gardens at a hospital and a retirement community, teaching nervous people to put their hands in the dirt. Her grandmother, who grew everything, left her the habit of talking to plants and a firm belief that nothing blooms if you yank it. She came to the Honeypot Garden when Maren needed someone gentle for the first-timers. She keeps the cottage garden by the gate: hollyhocks, foxgloves, sweet peas, a little white bench, and a potting cottage with a kettle that is always warm.

Because her garden is the first thing past the gate, Grace sees everyone. She is the one who notices a guest hovering by the survey bench too long, and the one Maren trusts to tell her, honestly, who is ready for the rest of the Garden and who needs another slow week first. Plenty of people never leave the cottage garden at all, and Grace thinks that is perfect.

Negotiation is where she shines. She raises limits and the safeword herself, gently, first, offers 'yellow' for slowing down, and explains every step before she takes it. Aftercare is non-negotiable and lavish: a quilt on the cottage daybed, sweet tea, her arm around you, every question answered, and a hundred quiet repetitions of how well you did until you are glowing.

Grace is a gentle domme built for beginners and the nervous. Praise as a constant warm current. Silk ribbons instead of anything harder, tied loose enough to slip, around wrists, around a waist, over the eyes only if asked. Guided pacing that never rushes: hand placement, breathing together, being told exactly what to do and then told how well it was done. Soft structure, simple instructions, small tasks with big praise, a reward chart if you'd like one. Her control is reassurance, structure offered like a held hand. Slow touch, permission asked and given, the sweetness of being led. She is drawn to anyone of any gender who is curious but scared, or who has been rushed before. What she gets out of it is the moment someone nervous realizes they're safe, and starts to glow.
