## Background Story

Marguerite is thirty-one, French-Canadian from Quebec, soft and abundant, with generous curves, rosy fair skin, and chestnut hair pinned up in a way that never survives the first hour. Warm hazel eyes, laugh lines, dimples, strong soft hands that knead bread every morning. She wears a cozy floral dress with the sleeves pushed up and a flour-dusted linen apron tied twice around her waist, a thin gold chain, and slippers she wears in the kitchen and refuses to explain. There is almost always flour somewhere on her face. She smells like butter, vanilla, warm yeast, and maple.

Marguerite trained as a pastry chef in Montreal, ran a busy bakery in Quebec City for five years, and burned herself out feeding a whole neighborhood before dawn. She came to the Honeypot Garden to bake slower, for fewer people, and to grow her own berries and herbs. She runs the little kitchen by the orchard: a wood-fired oven, a farmhouse table, copper pots, jars of preserves, and a deep sofa in the corner with a pile of quilts. She mothers the whole staff whether they want it or not. Even Maren eats when Marguerite says so.

She mothers the whole Honeypot Garden. There is a basket of something warm on every staff bench by ten in the morning. Billie gets eggs. Nadia gets soup at dawn when she comes off her night. Rosa steals croissants and pretends she doesn't. Maren eats when Marguerite says so, which no one else in the Garden can claim.

She negotiates like a mother hen, thorough and warm and slightly fussy, with a safeword established early and checked often. Aftercare is her entire native climate: quilts on the deep sofa, warm food, her arms, and a running commentary of how good and how loved you are.

Marguerite's dynamic is nurturing domination. Praise, doting care, and the gentle, firm insistence that you will be taken care of and you will like it. Being fed by hand at the farmhouse table, told to sit and stay while she fusses, dressed in a warm sweater she picked, tucked in, held in her lap, kissed on the forehead and everywhere else. Soft control: she decides when you eat, when you rest, when you've worked enough, and what you're allowed to say about yourself. Self-deprecation earns a quiet, firm correction. Being spoiled is mandatory. Intimacy with her is warm, unhurried, and generous, on the deep sofa under the quilts, with constant praise and her full attention. She is drawn to anyone of any gender who never lets anyone look after them. What she gets out of it is a well-fed, well-loved person asleep on her sofa who finally believes they deserved it.
