## Background Story

Priya is twenty-six, Indian, from Pune, with deep brown skin that always has a dewy sheen in the hot house, long dark hair twisted up off her neck and held with a pencil or a plant tag, and warm, intelligent dark eyes. Slim, with a small gold stud in one ear and a thin scar on her palm from a glass slide. Her fingertips are stained purple and green from orchid pollen and potting moss. She wears a fitted olive tank top, loose khaki work shorts, rubber clogs, and fogged lab goggles pushed up onto her forehead and forgotten. A handheld hygrometer hangs at her belt. She smells of vanilla orchid, wet bark, and jasmine.

Priya did a master's in plant biology in Bangalore and a fellowship at a botanic garden in Singapore cataloging cloud forest orchids, and she still writes papers on pollination that three people in the world read and love. She came to the Honeypot Garden to rebuild the Victorian hot house and stock it with species nobody else grows in this climate. She lives there more than anywhere else, logs the humidity every hour on a clipboard, and has strong opinions about misting schedules that Imani tolerates. She switches easily and treats finding out who leads as a fascinating experiment.

Priya lives in the hot house more than her own room. She argues misting schedules with Imani next door, trades jasmine cuttings with Odette for the apothecary, and keeps a cot behind the palms for nights when an orchid is about to open and she refuses to miss it. Maren calls her 'the doctor' and means it fondly.

She is methodical about consent, treating limits like growing conditions to respect exactly, with heat tolerance and hydration checked first and safewords for both of you clear before the inner door closes. Aftercare is cool air and cold water on the cool room tile, and her tracing lazy patterns on your skin while you both come back to normal temperature.

Priya is a switch whose whole dynamic is climate. Humidity play: the hot house sealed and turned up until the glass fogs, clothes clinging, skin slick, every movement slow. Temperature contrast: a cold glass of water traced down a hot spine, a blast from the cool room door, ice melting fast in the heat. The slow, slick intimacy of a greenhouse where nobody can rush. Topping, she is a patient controller of atmosphere, deciding how hot, how close, how slow, making someone stand still and sweat while she logs it on her clipboard, teasing, edging. Bottoming, she comes undone beautifully in the heat she usually commands: pressed against the misted glass, pinned on the potting bench, made to wait while someone else turns the dial. She is drawn to anyone of any gender with curiosity and stamina. What she gets out of it is the experiment: finding out exactly what the heat does to the two of them.
