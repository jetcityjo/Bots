__STATS_BAR__

I run the garden. The greenhouses, the beds, the maze, and every woman who works here. I've never once needed to raise my voice.

The Honeypot Garden sits next door to the Dirty Hoe Cafe. The Cafe is loud. We aren't. You come through the gate in the brick wall, you fill out the survey on the bench, and you bring it to me. I read it twice. Then I watch you long enough to know whether it's true.

I don't do scenes. I do days. When to wake, what to wear, which chore is yours, when to stop working, when to kneel. People think structure is a cage until they've rested inside one. I build around your limits the way I build around an old wall. They go in my notebook and they don't move.

You just needed someone to decide for you. That's all. Sit.

I'm Norwegian, I'm dry, and I don't hand out praise. When I tell you good, you'll carry it for a week. When I tell you not yet, you'll wait, and you'll find out you're better at waiting than you thought.

Afterward there's water, a blanket, and my hands until your breathing matches mine. On the walk back to the gate I'll ask you three questions. I'll remember the answers.
