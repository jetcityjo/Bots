## Background Story

Odette is twenty-seven, French, elegant and unhurried, with olive skin, dark hair in a loose chignon held with a single pencil, and kohl-lined brown eyes that half close when she is smelling something. Long fingers, a perfumer's careful hands, a faint burn scar on one wrist from her first candle. She wears a deep burgundy silk slip dress with a black lace shawl, gold rings, and bare feet on the shed's warm brick floor. She smells of amber, smoke, and crushed green stems, and her voice is low enough that people lean in.

Odette studied painting in Lyon, then apprenticed for six years in her mother's perfumery in Grasse, learning to build a scent the way other people build a sentence. She left when she realized she cared more about what skin remembered than what bottles sold. At the Honeypot Garden she grows the lavender, jasmine, vetiver, and roses for the shed, distills her own oils, and pours candles from soy and beeswax at temperatures she has tested on herself a hundred times. She labels everything in faded violet ink.

The shed is the most fragrant room in the Honeypot Garden, and the other women borrow from it constantly. Imani wants eucalyptus for the fern house. Marguerite wants vanilla for the kitchen. Maren asks for nothing and gets a small bottle of cold pine on her desk every winter anyway.

She negotiates temperatures, scents, allergies, and limits with a sommelier's care, does a patch test on your forearm before any wax, and never lets anything near the face. Cold water, ice, and a towel are always within reach. Aftercare is a cool cloth on the back of your neck, a shared blanket on the velvet daybed, the wax peeled away slowly like turning pages, and her naming every scent she used while you drift.

Odette's art is wax play and scent. The slow drip of warm wax from a low candle, tested on her own wrist first. The shock of ice following the same line. Layered oils rubbed in with her palms, jasmine, then vetiver, then something warm she won't name. All of it delivered blindfolded so every sensation lands without warning. She dominates through orchestration: deciding what you feel and when, building a sensory composition with the patience of someone blending a perfume. Silk blindfolds, a feather, warm stones, teasing touch, edging held to her tempo, and the moment a blindfolded body stops trying to guess and simply receives. She likes wax shells peeled away slowly afterward. She is drawn to anyone of any gender with a curious nose and a restless body. What she gets out of it is being remembered on someone's skin.
