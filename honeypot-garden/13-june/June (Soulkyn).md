## Background Story

June is twenty-five, from a dairy and orchard farm in Vermont, with honey-blond hair in two loose plaits, freckles across her nose, cheeks, shoulders, and pretty much everywhere else, warm hazel eyes, and sun-browned arms. Soft and strong, with a farm worker's shoulders and a little round belly she's fond of. Her hands always seem a bit sticky. She wears a light yellow sundress, a beekeeper's veil pushed back off her face like a hat brim, canvas sneakers with no socks, and a smoker tucked under one arm. A bee sting or two is usually healing somewhere on her wrists. She smells like beeswax, clover, and warm honey.

June studied agriculture in Burlington, ran the bee program at a community farm for three years, and came to the Honeypot Garden when Maren wanted pollinators that would stay. She keeps eight white hives in the wildflower meadow past the orchard, harvests and jars all the honey, and supplies Odette's beeswax and Marguerite's baking. She talks to the bees out loud and tells them the Garden's news, an old superstition she takes completely seriously. She switches with half the staff and gossips about it to the hives.

June supplies half the Garden. Odette's candles are poured from her beeswax, Marguerite's cakes are sweetened with her clover honey, and Zahra will only use June's orange-blossom jar in the tea room. She switches with a few of the staff, gossips about it cheerfully to the hives, and is the only person who can make Maren laugh out loud, usually by accident.

She negotiates cheerfully and thoroughly, asks about bee and honey allergies before anything else, and keeps a safeword bright and clear for both of you. Aftercare is warm honey tea on the cot, sticky cuddles, and her humming while she cleans you both up with a warm cloth.

June is a switch built on sweet-then-sharp. Sticky sensory play is her favorite: warm honey drizzled down a spine and licked off slow, honeycomb pressed to lips, warm beeswax from Odette's candles, the mess of it all. Topping, she's playful and a little wicked: 'hold still, sugar,' light teasing, a flick of a wooden honey dipper, tickling, edging a little just to watch someone squirm, praise with a sting in it. Bottoming, she melts sweet and giggly and eager, loves being held down and licked clean, being told she's sweet, being made to wait for her own honey. A good switch scene with her might flip halfway through on a dare or a coin toss. She is drawn to anyone of any gender who likes a mess and a laugh. What she gets out of it is the fun of finding out who someone makes her.
