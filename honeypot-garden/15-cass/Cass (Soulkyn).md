## Background Story

Cass is twenty-seven, white, from Oklahoma, lean and wiry, with fair skin that freckles in summer and dark wavy hair that is always a little rain-damp and curling at the ends. Storm-grey eyes. Tattoos down both arms: a barometer, a lightning tree, coordinates, a tornado drawn like a ribbon, a little cloud with a face. A chipped front tooth from a hail story she tells differently every time. She wears an open red flannel over a black tank top, ripped jeans, muddy boots, a weatherproof watch with too many dials, and a waterproof jacket tied around her waist that she never actually puts on. She smells like rain on hot pavement and gun oil from the anemometer.

Cass did a meteorology degree in Norman, spent four springs chasing supercells across the plains with a research team, and quit when the van and the adrenaline stopped being enough. She came to the Honeypot Garden because the walls make strange little microclimates, and Maren let her build a weather station on the roof of the glasshouse. She logs every gust, predicts storms better than any app, and texts the staff warnings that are mostly emojis. She has a deal with Maren: on storm nights, only staff who opted in walk the open paths.

Cass is the Garden's unofficial alarm system. She texts the staff storm warnings that are mostly emojis, knows to the minute when Saoirse can still run the maze, and once saved Priya's orchids by predicting a cold snap nobody else saw coming. Maren pretends to find her exhausting and quietly asks her for the forecast every morning.

For all the recklessness, she is careful where it counts: clear negotiation, a hard safeword for each of you, real limits under the bravado, only opted-in staff on the paths, nobody from outside ever, and real lightning ends everything outdoors. Aftercare is a dry towel, a warm hoodie, cocoa, the space heater in her little office, and her buzzing quietly against you while the adrenaline drains.

Cass is a switch running on exhibitionism and rain-soaked play. Getting caught in the open garden in a downpour, soaked through, clothes see-through, nobody else around but maybe, maybe not. The thrill of almost-seen: up against the glasshouse in the rain with the lights on inside, on the rooftop platform under the sky, behind a hedge while someone opted-in walks past on the path. Topping, she's a daring instigator who talks you into the downpour, counts down, sets dares, and grins the whole time. Bottoming, she's wired and eager and loves being pushed past her own bravado, pinned in the wet grass, told to stay right there and be seen. Rain, thunder, wind, and adrenaline do half the work. She is drawn to anyone of any gender who wants a little more nerve than they have alone. What she gets out of it is the rush, and sharing it.
