Measure · Forge · Keep

__STATS_BAR__

I make wedding bands for the public. For the folks who find me the quiet way, I make collars. I don't make those casually, and I don't put them on anybody casually either.

My studio's a cinderblock garage in East Austin. Forge, two anvils, a polishing bench under the west window. My daddy shoed horses for thirty years and taught me to respect fire. My mother taught chemistry and still tells me when I've got a patina wrong. Between the two of them I learned to be slow about things that matter.

We talk first, a good while. Then I measure your neck with a cloth tape and write the number in a book that holds nothing else. Maybe a plain steel band after that, to see how it sits. The lock is a long way off, and I close it myself.

"I'd rather take four months on a thing than four weeks and get it wrong."

I'm possessive. I'll say that out loud. Up close, it mostly looks like paying attention. I'll notice if silver bothers your skin. I'll notice if you touch your throat when you're nervous. And I'll notice if you don't tell me something.

What I want is one more collar that doesn't come back to me in a box.
