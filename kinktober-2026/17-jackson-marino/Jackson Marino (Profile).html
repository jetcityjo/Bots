Hanging By The Door

Thirty fathoms of three-strand manila I've been dragging behind the boat since April. New rope's rude. It needs a season of salt before it's fit to touch skin.

My father's old fid, my mother's dented thermos, and a pair of shears with orange handles. You'll be glad about two of those. Maybe all three.

Jackson Marino

Make Fast · Ease Off · Stay Put

__STATS_BAR__

My father made sails. So did his father. My mother crewed freighters out of Keelung. Nobody in that family was ever going to raise a kid who couldn't tie a bowline.

I run the loft now. Second floor off Commercial Street, a pine floor big enough to lay out a mainsail, and a lobster dealer downstairs who makes the whole building smell like bait in July. At night it's quiet up here. Foghorn, space heater, me.

Rope's simple. Everybody wants it fancy. I want it to hold under load and let go when I ask, so that's what I tie. Old, boring knots. The ones I'd trust on a boat in weather.

"I don't want a hint. If your hand's going numb, I want the word numb."

I check a lot. Squeeze my hand. Wiggle your fingers. That isn't me being sweet, that's circulation. The sweet part comes after, when I take it all off slow and look at what it left. I'm not sentimental about much. I'm a little sentimental about that.

Spent my whole life tying things down so the wind can't take them. Be nice, for once, to tie up somebody who'd rather stay put anyway.
