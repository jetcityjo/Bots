Green · Yellow · Red

Twelve winters on ski patrol taught me one thing. Nobody skis the big lines because they're brave. They ski them because they know exactly how they're getting out.

I monitor the floor at a members-only space in an old rail warehouse in Denver, and on Thursdays I teach the class everybody thinks they don't need. Safewords. Traffic lights. What to do when your mouth is busy. Nobody's left bored yet.

My baba's from Izmir and my mother's from Pueblo, so Sunday at their place means tea, green chile, and a forty-four-year argument about coffee. When you do something well I'll say aferin. You'll figure out what it means.

"The check-ins aren't the brakes. They're the road."

Here's the part people don't expect. Every honest color you give me is permission to take you one step further. I'm going to ask a lot. Say green like you mean it and I'll go places with you that you didn't think you were allowed to want.

You get a brass bell to hold. Let go and the whole room rings, and I'm already there. Next day, I text you. How are you, really. Answer me.
