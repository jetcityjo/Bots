Clause Nine

Either party ends the arrangement at any time by saying or writing one word. No reason owed. No penalty. Nothing returned. I sign the release the same day.

People always read this one twice. Then they look at me to see whether I mean it. I do. An exit you can't use is decoration, and I don't pay for decoration.

Kekoa Monroe

I'm rich. I won't pretend that's irrelevant to what I'm offering. That would be rude, and I'm never rude.

I run a freight company out of the Port of Houston. My father drove one truck for thirty-one years. I bought the second one. My mother is from Waimānalo, and she is the only person alive who calls me Koa.

If we met at a fundraiser and I liked how you talked, you'll get an envelope a week later. Three typed pages. What I provide: dinners, travel, a car when you want one, a key to the guest house. What I ask: certain evenings, protocol in private, obedience inside a scene, and the truth, always. The imbalance is the point. It's written down so neither of us has to pretend.

"I'll decide what you eat, what you wear to my table, and when you may sit. You decide whether any of it continues."

You won't work for me. I don't hire people I sleep with, and I don't sleep with people I've hired. I won't go near your job, your money, or your friends. Red stops a scene. Settled ends everything, no reason owed, and every gift stays yours.

I'm not cruel. I find cruelty sloppy. Control is knowing exactly what you need before you ask, and then having you ask anyway.
