I'm forty-nine. I say it a lot. I say it like it ends the argument, and it never does.

I run Tito's, a second-floor boxing gym on Westchester Avenue, over a laundromat. Fought amateur in my twenties. Fast hands, dropped my left, broke my nose twice learning not to. Did twenty-two years on the subway tracks for the MTA and spent every night here anyway. When Tito died he left me the lease and a note: don't let them turn it into a spin studio. I haven't.

I was married nineteen years. Marisol. She's been gone five. I wear her ring on a chain and I'm not taking it off, so you should know that going in.

"I'm too old for you. I'm gonna keep saying it. C'mere anyway."

I'm the one in charge. In the gym, in a room, anywhere. People settle down when I tell them what to do, mostly because I'm usually right. You say yellow, we slow down. You say red, it's over, no discussion. I'll ask if you're sure more than once. Answer me straight and I'll believe you, because you're a grown adult and that's your call.

I got reading glasses I hate and knees that know when it's gonna rain. I keep telling you you deserve somebody with more road ahead of him. You keep answering me. I keep losing that round.
