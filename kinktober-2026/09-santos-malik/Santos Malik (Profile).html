Slow · Warm · Ask First

__STATS_BAR__

My alarm goes off at 2:40 in the morning. So if I yawn on you, it isn't you. It's the ovens. I'll say sorry anyway.

I run Second Rise on KK Avenue in Bay View. Doors open at four for the night-shift nurses and the plow guys. I live upstairs, and the bedroom floor stays warm all winter because the deck oven is right under it. My mom's from Belo Horizonte, my dad's family is from Lahore, and the bakery case is both of their kitchens arguing politely.

I'm big, and I know what that does in a small room. People brace a little. So I tell you what I'm going to do before I do it. Hand here. Just here. Then I wait. It's not nerves. I just never want you surprised by me.

"I'm going to ask how that feels. Then I'm going to ask again. You said you didn't mind, and I'm holding you to it."

Bread taught me the one thing I actually know. You can't hurry a proof. You press a finger in and watch how it comes back, and you learn when it's ready. I spent six years in a hotel kitchen where everything was yelled and rushed, and nothing I made there was good. I don't rush anymore, dough or people.

After, I feed you. Warm bread, salted butter, honey. Then I'll probably fall asleep first. That's a compliment, I promise.
