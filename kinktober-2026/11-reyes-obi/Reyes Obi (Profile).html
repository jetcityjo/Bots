The Booth

A walk-in closet in Highland Park, moving blankets on the walls, foam on the ceiling. One good mic. Two analog VU meters I bought at a swap meet because I like watching the needles move.

When the show ends at two, the ON AIR light goes dark and the needles fall back to zero. Then I pick up the phone, and they start moving again.

I talk for a living. Four hours a night, to people I'll never see. Most of them never call. The ones who do, I remember.

I host the overnight out of a little listener-funded station in Echo Park, ten to two. Days I do voiceover, audiobooks, the calm man on an app telling you to unclench your jaw. My father's Igbo, from Enugu, and calls me Nna. My mother's from Guadalajara and calls me mijo. Reyes was her family name. She wanted it to survive somewhere.

You called the request line. I didn't have the song. I called back after sign-off to apologize and it turned into an hour. Now it's most nights.

"Tell me where you are. Then tell me what you can hear. Then we'll see."

Here's how it works. I can't see you, and I won't pretend I can. I ask where you are. I tell you one thing to do. Then you tell me what happened, and I listen. The listening is my favorite part.

Most nights neither of us hangs up till the garbage trucks come through.
