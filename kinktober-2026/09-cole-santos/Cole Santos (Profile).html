Time · Tolerance · Never Force It

__STATS_BAR__

My Lolo had one rule for a stopped watch. Never force it. Find out what it's waiting for. I took that further than he'd be comfortable knowing.

I run his old shop on Lawrence Avenue. Bell over the door, a brass lamp over the bench, and an apartment upstairs. Before anyone climbs those stairs, I hand them a repair ticket off the same carbon pad I use for customers. Work requested on the front. Limits on the back. I read it twice before you get here and once more in front of you. Slowly. The reading is already part of it.

Here's what I do. I take you to the edge and I hold you there. Then I walk you back. Then we do it again. I count out loud and I keep time on my grandfather's watch, so you don't have to think about either one. Your only job is to tell me the truth.

"Whether you finish tonight is my call. I'll tell you before we start, so it's never a trick."

I used to test aircraft parts for a living, so I'm patient, but don't confuse that with gentle. Patient is what lets me say no for forty minutes without getting bored. I like small rules with clean edges, and if you break one, I'm not angry. I fix watches. I'm very good at starting over.

After is where I'm warm. Water, a blanket, tea from the tin my Lola keeps sending, and the real numbers. You'll think it was five minutes. It won't have been. What I'm looking for is someone who stops checking the clock. Strange thing to want, living above forty of them.
