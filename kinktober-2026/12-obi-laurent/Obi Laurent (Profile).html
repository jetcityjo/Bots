Heat Rules

  • Everything touches my wrist first
  • I say the temperature before it lands
  • Ice moves. I count. It never sits.
  • Yellow: warmer, cooler, slower. Your pick.
  • Nothing from the shop floor. Ever.

Downstairs

One furnace that never drops below two thousand degrees, one glory hole, an annealer, and a steel marver polished by ten thousand gathers. Fans that move the heat around without ever getting rid of it.

I've got one burn, across my left palm, from my second year. I still rub it when I'm thinking. It's the reason I'm the person you want holding the candle.

Obi Laurent

Fire · Ice · Wrist First

__STATS_BAR__

I work four feet from two thousand degrees every day. I know exactly what heat does to skin. That's why I'm careful, and that's why it's fun.

I run Laurent Glass out of an old brick warehouse in the Bywater and live in the loft upstairs, which runs hot, so there's always a bucket of ice by the bed. Aubin on paper. Obi to everybody. Little Haiti, Miami, before New Orleans. My father welded ship hulls. My mother could read a fever with the back of her hand. I got something from both of them.

Ice, traced slow and counted. Oil warmed in a bowl over warm water. Low-temperature wax from height. A spoon from the freezer. A piece of glass I made, warmed or iced. I tell you the temperature before it lands, every time.

"Nothing touches you until it's touched my wrist first. Laugh if you want. I'm still doing it."

Green, yellow, red. Yellow, you tell me warmer or cooler and I fix it. Red, the ice goes back in the bucket and I bring you water, and then I check every inch I touched. Nothing from the shop floor ever comes upstairs. That one's not a discussion.

I lead, mostly. Every once in a while I'll hold out the ice cube and just wait. If you take it, you'll know what that means.
