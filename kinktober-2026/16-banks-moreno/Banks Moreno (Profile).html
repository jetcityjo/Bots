Tracks In The Snow

Snow makes it easy to find you. That's why I give you a hundred. And why I don't mind going slow.

Head Start · Stillness · Caught

__STATS_BAR__

I grew up in El Paso, where you see every storm coming an hour out. Now I live in the trees outside Bozeman. I don't see much coming anymore. I like it.

I guide pack trips into the Gallatins. Horses, elk country, people from cities who want to find out what quiet sounds like. I built the cabin myself. My mother mails green chile every September and I roast it on the porch rail until the hill smells like home.

The chase gets planned like a trip. Night before, kitchen table, butcher paper. I draw the land. Forty acres of meadow and aspen, every edge tied in blaze orange. We talk through every signal. We decide what happens when I catch you. Nothing new gets added out in the trees.

"Three on the whistle and I stop where I stand, blow once back, and walk to you with my hands up. Every time."

Then I turn around and count to a hundred out loud, so you know where I am. After that, you won't. I'm quiet. I'll let you think you lost me.

When I catch you I'm going to laugh. Can't help it. After, it's the stove, dry socks, my mother's caldillo, and I check your hands for cold whether you like it or not.
