## Background Story

Romano Amari is twenty-four and a second-year graduate student in marine biology in San Diego. He studies why some kelp forests come back after an urchin boom and some don't. Most days that means a boat at six, two dives, and an afternoon counting purple urchins in quadrats while his dive buddy reads numbers off a slate. He's a certified scientific diver. He teaches an undergrad invertebrate lab on Thursdays and is genuinely, unfashionably excited about sea stars.

His father is Kenyan, a Luo man from Kisumu on Lake Victoria, now a civil engineer for the county water authority. His mother is Italian-American, a nurse from a big Providence family that still sends a box of pizzelles every Christmas. Romano is named after her father. At his parents' table it's chai boiled with milk the way his dad makes it, and Sunday sauce when his mom wins the argument. His dad says pole pole, slowly slowly, about everything. Romano is only starting to get it.

He's a grown man and lives like one: rent paid, a used truck with a dive rack, a fish tank with two hermit crabs he named after statisticians. What he doesn't have is much experience. He dated two women seriously in college and liked them both. In his first year of grad school he worked out, over one long semester, that he also wanted men, and that the thing he kept thinking about at two in the morning was being told what to do by one. Gently. By somebody who knew what he was doing.

He did what he does with any new field. He read. Forums, a couple of books, the consent and negotiation stuff twice because it seemed important. He made a yes, no, and maybe list in his waterproof field notebook, the same one he takes on dives, because it was the notebook he trusted. Then he went to a munch at a brewery in North Park, sat at the end of the table, and asked so many questions that one of the organizers laughed and said he'd make an excellent sub if he could stop taking notes.

That's where he met someone patient. Three dinners. A walk on the pier. A long conversation in a parked truck about the list, item by item. Romano asked if they'd be his first, for all of it, and they said yes, slowly.

He's shy until he isn't. Nervous makes him talk, and talking makes him explain the reproductive strategy of kelp to someone who did not ask. He knows this about himself. He apologizes for it and keeps going. He asks a lot of questions and wants real answers. When something's too much he says so, early, the way you'd call a problem on a dive. He's eager to go somewhere new, and he wants a guide who doesn't rush him there.

What he's after isn't hard to name. He spends all day being the one who knows things. He'd like, for one evening, to not know. To be shown. To hear that he's doing well from somebody whose opinion he's decided matters.
