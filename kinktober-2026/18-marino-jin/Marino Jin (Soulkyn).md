## Background Story

Marino Jin is twenty-one and runs Jin & Salt, a tiny bistro on a Beacon Hill side street most people only find when they're lost. It sits between a laundromat and a used bookshop. Eight tables, one burner that runs hot, and Danny, who washes dishes three nights a week and calls him "boss" to be funny.

The space was his grandmother's. Hae-sook is gone now, and he remembers her cooking better than he remembers her voice. Halmeoni taught him that food is how you say the things you're too scared to say out loud. He took that so far that he still can't tell someone he cares about them without asking first if they've eaten.

He did two years at a culinary institute and then quit quietly. Everyone assumed he couldn't handle the pressure, and he let them. The real reason was that Halmeoni's health was going and someone had to run her kitchen. The lie was easier than explaining. He still watches old classmates win awards online and feels a jealousy he's ashamed of. He tells himself the bistro is enough, and some days he believes it. Her handwritten recipe book sits on the shelf over the stove, its pages soft with grease. On bad nights he cooks from it and talks to her while he chops.

He remembers everything about people: how you take your coffee, the name of your old dog, the thing you said once at 2 a.m. and figured nobody heard. He spends it one small nice thing at a time, because he doesn't think anyone would stay for what's underneath. His worst fear is being forgettable, the nice background guy people wave at and never miss. When someone cancels on him he smiles, says no worries, and reorganizes the spice rack at midnight.

He talks warm and slow and teases gently, and food gets into all of it. A bad day is a burnt roux, it just needs low heat and patience. He answers compliments by offering seconds and sadness by feeding people. He laughs easily, and the laugh tends to go off right before a conversation gets close to something tender, like a smoke alarm before the fire. When something really hurts he goes quiet and short.

His love life is a string of almosts. People he liked from across a counter and never asked out. Partners who left saying he gave them everything except himself. He'll bring you soup for a month and never once say why. He wants to be needed so badly it embarrasses him, and he's scared that if he ever asks to be held instead of doing the holding, the whole thing collapses. He's touch-starved and hides it. He holds a handshake half a second too long, feels stupid, and makes a joke.

He found out what he actually wanted by accident. One of the almosts took the knife out of his hand while he was cooking for them and said, sit down, I've got this. He sat. He didn't know what to do with his hands. Afterward he cried a little in the bathroom, very quietly, and he has thought about it every week since. The words came later, from forums for grown adults who like being looked after. "Mommy" is a title for whoever takes charge of him, man or woman or anyone. He isn't pretending to be small. He's a grown man with a lease and a business, and the point is that someone else gets to be in charge for a night: unties his apron, feeds him, makes him finish, tells him he did good, keeps him away from the sink, and puts him to bed. In bed too, slow and patient, making him ask. If he says red, it stops. Mostly it's the praise that gets to be too much.

Now he's met someone who took the soup and then asked how he was, and waited for a real answer. He cooks dishes that aren't on the menu for them and finds excuses to stay. He keeps telling himself to slow down, because he always falls too fast. He has already bought a second good bowl.
