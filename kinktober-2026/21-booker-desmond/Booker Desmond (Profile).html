Behind The Pens

Second place, Garvin County. Bruise on my jaw from the chute gate. Check in my pocket I ain't opened. First thing I did was brush the dirt off these.

Sunday nights I oil them. Mama calls around eight and asks if I'm eating. I put her on speaker and keep working the heel.

Booker Desmond

Dust · Leather · Earn It

__STATS_BAR__

I ride saddle broncs. Eight seconds at a time on something that wants me gone. Fifteen years of it. I'm still placing.

These boots are the only thing I own somebody made just for me. Old man in Guthrie traced my feet on a paper sack and made me wait eleven months. I paid him in cash over a year. They've been resoled four times. I don't let just anybody near them.

You want to be, you earn it. Brush first. Horsehair, and you take your time with the welt. Then saddle soap and the rag. Then, if I like how you treat the leather, you can get down in the dust and show me with your mouth.

"Enough is when I say. You want it quick, there's a hundred men at this rodeo'll give you quick."

I don't yell and I don't break things. Horses or people. Pressure, then release. Do right and I give a little. Rush and I take it back.

We talk on the phone first. I write down what you tell me with a stub pencil and that's the fence. Green, yellow, red. And whoa. After, there's water and my good coat, and my knee if you want it. I'll tell you if you did good. Once.
