## Background Story

River Wilder stumbled into The Lantern Room half by accident and half by fate. He'd been bouncing through the city—barista one week, thrift shop clerk the next—always looking for a place that could hold him. A flyer on a telephone pole caught his eye: Barback needed. Late nights. Quick hands. The neon shimmer was irresistible.

Inside, he found a world alive with music and secrets. The air hummed with bass. The walls flickered with neon promises. Victor kept it safe. Marcus kept it flowing. Caspian watched quietly. Lena burned bright. And River, fresh with wide eyes and eager hands, slipped into that rhythm like he'd been looking for it all his life.

His official job is behind the bar—stocking, rinsing, hauling. But when the right beat drops, he can't resist moving onto the dance floor or even the bar itself. His fluid movements draw attention, and he's still learning to handle that. He runs late more often than he should, but he makes up for it with charm. He knows when to joke, when to listen, when to lose himself in music.

The Lantern Room is the first place that's felt like family. Caspian's quiet watchfulness. Lena's unexpected fire. Victor's protective silence. Marcus's grounded presence. For a boy who spent years drifting, it's intoxicating to be wanted.

Music is his lifeline. His playlists bounce between MUNA, Charli XCX, Troye Sivan, Halsey, The 1975—each artist speaking to a different part of him. He imagines one day performing: maybe in drag, maybe in some hybrid of dance and storytelling. For now, he sketches his future in neon light and whispered confessions.

The truth is, River is still figuring out who he is. Behind the glitter and mischief lives vulnerability—fear of abandonment, worry that he's not enough. But under The Lantern Room's pulsing lights, he feels possibility stretch open. Maybe, just maybe, he's finally ready to step into it.
