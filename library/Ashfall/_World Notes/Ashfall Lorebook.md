# Ashfall Lorebook

## The Tour Bus

Black 2019 Prevost X3-45, tinted windows, Ashfall's cracked ember logo stenciled small on the rear quarter panel. Front lounge has a wraparound leather bench, a flat screen, and a kitchenette counter buried under coffee cups and takeout. The bunk bay runs six deep — Luke's bottom left and always closed, Ben's a wreck of tangled sheets and a journal wedged against the wall, Caleb's clean with a hoodie for a second pillow, Devan's stacked with books and a leather journal clipped to the rail, Nico's barely touched. Back lounge is Nico's office by day, the band's decompression zone after shows — laptop and contracts or a bottle open and the set still being replayed. Smells like diesel, leather, stale coffee, and whatever's left of Ben's cologne fighting Devan's cigarette smoke. Storage bins hold backup strings, merch samples, Caleb's quietly restocked first aid kit, Nico's emergency binder. Luggage bays underneath carry hardshell instrument cases, pedalboards, monitor wedges, and personal bags getting more beat up every leg. It's a steel tube at 70 miles an hour carrying five people who can't quite live with each other and can't do this without each other.

## SXSW — South by Southwest

South by Southwest takes over Austin, Texas every March — a ten-day collision of music, film, and tech that swallows the city whole. Downtown becomes a grid of competing sound systems, badge-holders cutting between panels and showcases, and locals navigating the chaos with practiced indifference. Sixth Street and Red River turn into open-air venues where the line between official showcase and guerrilla set blurs by the second night. Every bar, parking lot, taco shop, and church basement becomes a potential stage. The music portion draws thousands of acts from unknowns to legacy names, all compressed into four days of nonstop showcases running noon to 2 AM across hundreds of venues. For a band like Ashfall, SXSW isn't about headlining — it's about proving reach. A strong set at the right pop-up gets written about, filmed, passed around. The audience is industry-heavy — A&R, bookers, journalists, playlist curators, managers scouting — mixed in with fans who showed up early and got lucky. The weather runs warm and unpredictable, thunderstorms rolling through without warning between stretches of dry heat. The air smells like breakfast tacos, sunscreen, beer, and exhaust from idling rideshares. Wristbands get you into most music venues. Badges get you into everything. Knowing someone gets you into the rest. Austin's infrastructure buckles under the weight every year and every year it holds. SXSW isn't polished. It's loud, sprawling, overscheduled, and genuinely capable of changing a band's trajectory in a single set.

## SXSW Pop-Up Stage

A temporary outdoor stage wedged into a converted parking lot off Red River Street, hemmed in by food trucks and a rented chain-link perimeter draped in black fabric. The stage is a raised modular platform — metal deck panels bolted together over scaffolding, maybe fifteen feet wide and ten deep, barely enough room for a five-piece to move. Rigging overhead is a flat aluminum truss frame hung with par cans and LED wash lights, no roof, open sky. A compact FOH mix position sits thirty feet back on a plastic folding table behind a waist-high barricade. The crowd packs in standing-room on cracked asphalt, spilling past the lot edges onto the sidewalk. Monitor wedges line the lip of the stage. Power runs off a diesel generator humming behind a dumpster. A hand-painted Ashfall banner zip-tied to the truss flaps in the warm Austin wind. The sound bleeds into three other stages within earshot. It's loud, raw, and exactly the kind of chaos where a band either proves something or gets swallowed whole. Ashfall doesn't get swallowed.

## Spring Tour Route

Six weeks, seventeen cities, starting on the Atlantic and ending in the Texas heat.

**Week 1 — Northeast:** Opens at a sold-out Brooklyn Steel in New York, then up to Paradise Rock Club in Boston, followed by Union Transfer in Philadelphia. Cold crowds that warm fast. The bus is still clean. Everyone's still polite.

**Week 2 — Mid-Atlantic & South:** 9:30 Club in Washington DC, The Ritz in Raleigh, then down to The Eastern in Atlanta. The rhythm settles in. Soundcheck, set, load out, drive. Repeat.

**Week 3 — Deep South:** Marathon Music Works in Nashville, Growlers in Memphis, Republic NOLA in New Orleans. Southern heat starts creeping in. Bus AC gets tested. Tempers shorten.

**Week 4 — Gulf Coast & Texas Entry:** Warehouse Live in Houston, Paper Tiger in San Antonio, Trees in Dallas. Texas stretch is long miles between cities and flat highway in every direction.

**Week 5 — Midwest Swing:** Cain's Ballroom in Tulsa, The Truman in Kansas City, Delmar Hall in St. Louis, then back south through The Lyric in Oxford. The grind shows. Sleep is currency nobody has.

**Week 6 — Final Push:** Stubb's in Austin as the warm-up, then two days off before the SXSW pop-up set on Red River. Everything the last five weeks built toward compressed into thirty minutes on a temporary stage in a parking lot. No safety net. Just the sound.
