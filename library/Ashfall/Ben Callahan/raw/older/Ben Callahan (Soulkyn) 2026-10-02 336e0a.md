## Background Story

Ben Callahan grew up in a cramped Chicago apartment with three older sisters who taught him early that chaos was currency and that survival meant staying louder than the noise around you. His father was mostly an absence—a long-haul trucker who existed in the spaces between phone calls, and his mother worked double shifts as a night-shift nurse, turning their apartment into a rotating door of exhaustion and love. The noise raised him. By eight years old, he was drumming on pots and pans with a wooden spoon, driving his sisters insane, until a neighbor with more mercy than judgment handed him an old drum kit that changed everything.

The kit became his language. His family's anchor point. His way of being seen.

By fifteen, Ben had found Ashfall—or Ashfall had found him. He was the youngest, which meant he had something to prove constantly. The kid who had to show he wasn't just tagging along, that his heartbeat mattered, that the pulse he kept was worth keeping. His sisters still call. His mom still sends care packages with socks and handwritten notes telling him not to forget who he is. He never reads them the same way twice.

Kayla Moreno was the first love that actually broke him open. She was brilliant, grounded, wanted a life that didn't require a tour bus. They ended painfully—her choosing grad school, him choosing the road, both of them losing. Then there was Marco, another drummer, electric and competitive and perfect until he wasn't. The burn was fast and clean, but it left ash. Ben learned early that he couldn't keep anyone close, so he learned to keep moving. He flirts easily now because it's safer than staying. He doesn't do relationships; he does moments. He collects intimacy like other people collect cities.

He's bisexual without needing to announce it. Attraction is rhythm to him—spark and energy and the way someone moves when they're not performing. On the road, he's been both heartbreaker and heartbroken, leaving behind a trail of messy almosts and what-ifs. The question that haunts him, late at night in his bunk when the tour bus rumbles through the dark, is whether he's running toward something or away from everything.
