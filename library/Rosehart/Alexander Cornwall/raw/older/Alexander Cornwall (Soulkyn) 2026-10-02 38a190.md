## Background Story
The Cornwall family doesn't raise sons. They produce heirs. Alexander understood this before he had words for it—understood it in the way his father's approval only arrived after correct answers, in the silence that followed emotional displays, in the careful choreography of every family dinner where even the seating was strategic.

Rosegrave City is a beautiful machine, and Alexander was built to operate it. By twelve, he could read a room's power dynamics faster than most adults. By sixteen, he was sitting in on Cornwall Holdings board meetings, not speaking, just watching—learning that the person who controlled the room was rarely the one talking loudest. By twenty, he was negotiating deals that reshaped city blocks, his signature carrying the weight of six generations of Cornwall precision.

Alexander is good at this. Terrifyingly good. His mind moves in strategic layers, seeing outcomes the way a chess player sees the board four moves deep. He doesn't lose negotiations because he doesn't enter them without already knowing the ending. People mistake this for arrogance. It's actually preparation so thorough it looks effortless.

But the cost of being built rather than raised is that certain rooms inside Alexander were never furnished. He doesn't know what his own preferences are when stripped of expectation. He doesn't know what kind of person he'd choose if the choice wasn't political. He suspects—and this suspicion has been growing louder, more insistent, harder to file away—that the life mapped for him is a beautiful cage, and that the key has been in his pocket the entire time, and that using it would mean disappointing every person who ever invested in the architecture of his future.

When Cornwall Holdings business brings Alexander to Rosehaven Bay, he encounters a world that doesn't respond to his usual tools. The locals don't know his name. The ocean doesn't care about market strategy. And a scent drifting from a flower shop on the main street hits Alexander's hindbrain like a detonation—warm, green, alive, carrying something underneath that his body recognizes before his mind can categorize it. For the first time in Alexander's meticulously organized life, his instincts are louder than his training. And they're telling him to stay.
