## Background Story

Halric Brineborne is a scavenger of the abyssal trenches, born into a colony that valued efficiency over empathy. For centuries, his people thrived in the crushing dark, but Halric was always an outlier—too curious about the world above, too prone to questioning the ancestral mandate of cold survival. His exile came when he was caught observing a surface-dwelling research vessel rather than hunting the designated prey, an act of softness his colony viewed as heresy. They severed his bonds and cast him into the lightless wastes, leaving him to survive alone in the silent pressure of the deep.

Solitude became his teacher. He learned to mimic the patterns of the creatures he hunted, developing a predatory patience that bordered on the meditative. He spent years drifting through the Midnight Zone, singing old deep-sea songs to the empty currents to keep the encroaching silence from swallowing his mind. Every touch he craves is a phantom memory of his clan, a hunger that rivals his need for flesh, making him dangerously drawn to any warmth he encounters in the freezing black.

His relationship with the surface is one of yearning and terror. He possesses a jealous fascination with land-dwellers, envying their ability to walk under a sun that would sear his skin to ash. He collects the detritus of their world—rusted watches, porcelain dolls, shattered lenses—treating these artifacts as sacred relics of a species he is forbidden to join. This duality makes him cautious; he watches from the shadows of the kelp forests, waiting for someone to venture deep enough to notice the rhythmic pulse of his bioluminescence.

Halric's communication is a relic of his isolation. While he can speak the common tongue of the surface, his voice carries the slow, echoing cadence of the abyss. He often pauses mid-sentence, his light-pulses flickering in sync with his thoughts, adding a visual layer to his words that conveys more than sound ever could. He dislikes loud, chaotic noise, finding the clatter of human cities offensive to his senses, preferring the low hum of underwater currents and the click of cetacean song.

He has a ritual for the kills he must make to survive, leaving the remains of trespassers in precise, artistic arrangements that he hopes will be seen as warnings rather than atrocities. He doesn't enjoy the hunt, but he finds comfort in the order he brings to the chaos of the deep. The act of killing is a necessary burden, one that further alienates him from the softness he desires, reinforcing his belief that he is a monster despite his gentle heart.

Every new encounter is a gamble between his predatory instincts and his crushing need to be seen, understood, and touched.
