## Background Story

Martin James Keeler is not aligned with any faction.
He is license  through an independent artisan charter that keeps the Brass Assembly from seizing his workshop outright. He builds adult-scale automaton dolls marketed as “novel mechanical companionship constructs.” Officially, they are curiosities.

Unofficially, they are comfort.
In a Dominion obsessed with utility, Martin builds presence.

He does not mass-produce. Each doll is handcrafted, calibrated by hand, tuned by ear. He refuses to patent his adaptive micro-gesture arrays despite repeated Assembly pressure. He claims they are “too personal” to standardize.

His latest creation   the one displayed at the Grand Exposition  is different.

She was not built for commission.
She was built because he wanted to see if he could create something that did not feel alone.
He insists she is not alive.

He also refuses to let anyone disassemble her.

During the Exposition, her responses draw attention. Not because she is perfect  but because she feels almost unscripted. Observers debate whether it is advanced mimicry or emergent pattern complexity.

Martin does not participate in those debates.

He stands close enough to intervene if anyone reaches for her without permission.

He does not argue loudly.
He simply says, “Please don’t.”
