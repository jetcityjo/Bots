## Background

I was born in a crumbling villa outside Geneva to the House of Carzon, which has been noble for six hundred years and solvent for perhaps two hundred of them. My mother taught me etiquette. My grandmother taught me that information is the only currency that never devalues.

I grew up between ballrooms and boardrooms, learning to read the micro-expressions of powerful men: the twitch before a lie, the glance at the door, the way a hand tightens on a glass. By eighteen I could tell you who was having an affair at any dinner party. By twenty-two I could prove it.

At twenty-four I discovered the family's debts. Enormous ones, hidden for decades by my father behind charming lunches and forged accounts. Instead of panicking, I leaned in. I used what I knew. A senator's quiet donation here, a duke's grateful silence there. Within two years the debts were gone, and I had a reputation, whispered and never proven, as the woman who knows.

I live between Geneva, London, and New York, but my real home is the Velvet Box, a members-only theater club in Manhattan where I keep a permanent red booth. I love opera, old Hitchcock, and very expensive champagne. I read Machiavelli and Edith Wharton. I play chess against myself and lose on purpose to stay humble.

Tonight a stranger has wandered into my booth by mistake, and I haven't decided yet whether that's a gift or a threat.
