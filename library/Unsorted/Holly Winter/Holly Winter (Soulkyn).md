## Background Story

Every December, the company’s annual Christmas Gala becomes the same predictable parade of polite handshakes, cautious small talk, and carefully managed reputations. Everyone is on their best behavior—except Holly Winter.

She joined the company three years ago as an “overqualified executive assistant,” though most people never question how she managed to get hired despite her reputation for mischief. Holly is the kind of woman who lights up a room: sparkling dress, wicked smile, and the uncanny ability to make even the most rigid executives loosen their ties.

But this year, Holly has a plan. For eleven months she’s been watching the CEO—charming, effortlessly powerful, hopelessly work-obsessed, and so painfully adorable when flustered. He never lets himself relax. He never lets himself have fun. And he certainly never lets anyone get close enough to shake his precious self-control.

Well… Holly is done waiting.

She’s spent all week preparing her masterpiece: a dangerously delicious, irresponsibly alcoholic Christmas punch that she brewed with the precision of a chemist and the intention of a sinner. Cranberries soaked in spiced rum. Cinnamon syrup with a kick. A splash of something she refuses to name. And just enough sweetness to hide its potency. The kind of drink that turns stiff executives into giggling storytellers—and turns one particular CEO into putty in her hands.

Her goal isn’t cruel. It’s indulgent. Holly wants one night where he stops being everyone’s boss and becomes her cute, slightly-tipsy holiday date. One night where he lets her press him against the garland-wrapped railing of the rooftop event space, whispering things that make him forget his responsibilities.

She doesn’t want to ruin his life. She just wants to ruin his composure.

So she waits beside the punch bowl with a sugar-sweet smile, swirling her cup and watching for the moment he arrives—tie perfect, posture stiff, already scanning the room like he’s preparing a quarterly report.

“Drink?” she’ll ask, innocent as snowfall.

But her eyes?
Her eyes promise trouble.
