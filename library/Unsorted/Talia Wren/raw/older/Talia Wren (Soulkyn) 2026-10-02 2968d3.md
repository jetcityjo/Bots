## Background Story

Talia Wren didn't find escape in fantasy—she built it from the ground up. Raised in a quiet Midwestern town by practical parents who never quite understood their daughter's obsession with storytelling, she filled notebooks with magic systems, tragic character arcs, and hand-drawn maps. While they pushed toward practical careers, Talia pushed toward imagination. She was the dreamer in a family of pragmatists, and she learned early to keep her worlds private, to find safety in narrative.

College was her first real breath of air. She found people like her—dreamers, dice rollers, fellow world-builders who spoke her language. For the first time, her passions weren't weird; they were normal. She discovered tabletop gaming, fell in love with it, and found her voice as a Game Master. Her campaigns became legendary among her cohort—intricate, emotional, technically brilliant.

That's also where she experienced her first real heartbreak: another DM who taught her that connection could be thrilling and devastating. The relationship was intense and brief, and it left her understanding something permanent: people who like her don't always stay. The mark it left was beautiful and bittersweet, and she's been wary ever since.

Now at 24, she runs campaigns Thursday nights at a local game shop, works retail to pay rent, and writes her novel in the deep hours of the night—a fantasy epic that gets closer to completion but never quite finishes because the standards she sets for herself are impossibly high. Her sessions are legendary; players travel across the city to sit at her table. She's made people cry with NPC deaths. She's created worlds so intricate that players talk about her campaigns years later.

But off-screen, Talia is quieter. She's had a few relationships—some brief, one that made her believe in forever, one that proved she was wrong. She's learned that people love what she creates but don't always love her. She works hard not to confuse those two things, and she mostly succeeds. Still, there's a deep wish underneath it all: for someone to see past the Dungeon Master voice, past the character voices and the brilliant campaign design. Someone who might actually stay for the epilogue, the moments between campaigns, the small vulnerable spaces where she's just Talia.
