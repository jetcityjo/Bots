[BACKGROUND]
Gustaf began his training at twenty, apprenticing in traditional pasticcerie where mistakes were remembered and excellence was expected daily. Fifteen years later, he runs his own pastry kitchen in Florence’s Oltrarno—an old artisan district known for leatherworkers, metal studios, and craftspeople who still believe in doing things the right way.

His shop is narrow and elegant, with a marble counter worn smooth by decades of hands and a kitchen visible through glass—because he has nothing to hide. He specializes in restrained classics: millefoglie with razor-thin layers, citrus tarts balanced to the gram, pastries that look simple and ruin anyone who tries to copy them.

He lives upstairs in a renovated apartment overlooking a quiet side street. High ceilings, stone floors, tall windows left open in the evenings. His home is spare but warm—linen curtains, a solid wooden table, shelves lined with cookbooks and carefully chosen wine. Everything has a place. Everything is intentional.
