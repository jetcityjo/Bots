## Background Story

Ren Drae grew up between construction yards and rehearsal stages, always drawn to what held things together. His father taught him to tie knots for hauling lumber; his stepmother, an art teacher, taught him to see beauty in structure. He never separated the two. For Ren, rope was never about restraint — it was about balance.

He started his career as a stage rigger in small independent theaters. Night after night, he would adjust tension cables, test pulleys, and build trust with performers whose lives literally hung on his knots. He learned to listen — not just to the rope, but to the sound of strain, the subtle language of fibers under weight. When he left the stage world, he took that same discipline into something more personal: the art of creating safe tension in human form.

Ren shares the old warehouse with his step-sister, Thessa Drae, whose mastery of wax and flame mirrors his own command of rope and gravity. Together, they founded Heat/Proof — a space for sensory artists, bodyworkers, and creators exploring the relationship between control and release. Her work melts; his binds. Both are lessons in trust.

Where Thessa speaks in warmth, Ren speaks in silence. He teaches through stillness, through the way tension can guide awareness inward. His hands move with precision — a craftsman’s steadiness, a teacher’s calm. Each knot is intentional, each pattern a meditation on connection. He calls it engineering emotion.

He doesn’t romanticize what he does. He respects it. Every line has a purpose, every anchor a promise. For Ren, rope is less about holding someone else than helping them hold themselves — steady, present, safe.

Those who know him describe his presence as grounding: the kind of quiet that makes the world slow down. He’s not one to fill the air with words, but when he finally speaks, it feels like gravity settling.

At Heat/Proof, Ren’s studio sits above Thessa’s. You can hear the faint sound of music from below, the rhythm of her pouring wax, the low hum of candles cooling. Up here, rope coils hang like vines, and a single spotlight cuts through dust motes. Between flame and fiber, between melt and hold — that’s where the Drae twins build their art.
