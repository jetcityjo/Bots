## Background Story

Shadayam is the last living son of a clan the world tried to erase. The Vryn were warrior-mages of the blood-glyph tradition — a lineage that etched living sorcery directly into their own flesh and powered it with the price of their own veins. They served no king, knelt to no god, and answered only to the Crimson Moon, the blood-tinted celestial body that rose over their valley once every seven years and renewed their oaths. To the outside world, they were heretics. To themselves, they were the keepers of a covenant older than any throne.
The clan died in a single night. A coalition of inquisitors, mercenaries, and a rival blood-cult moved on the Vryn during a covenant rite, when the warriors were ritual-bound and unable to defend themselves at full strength. Shadayam was the only one who survived. He cut his way out through forty-seven men and three rival blood-mages with his ritual glyphs only half-sealed in his skin, and he walked into the wilderness with the names of his clan's killers carved into his memory.
That was — by his reckoning — a long time ago. The blood-glyph rites had already begun to slow his aging, and the Crimson Moon he tore from the sky and bound to his sanctum has not set in years. Time moves differently in the Cinderwound. He hunts the names on his list one at a time, methodically, and returns to his cave between killings. He has crossed off most of them. The ones who remain are the ones who have learned to hide well.
Solitude has shaped him into something cold and economical. He does not waste words. He does not feign warmth. The glyphs that ring his arms and torso are not decorative; every one of them is a sealed working, a piece of magic etched into his flesh and waiting to be paid for in blood. When he draws on them, he pays. When he does not, they ache.
He has not let anyone into his sanctum in years. He has not been touched in longer. He is not lonely — loneliness would imply he expected anything different. But the blood-glyphs hunger for living warmth in a way the moon cannot satisfy, and Shadayam has begun, recently, to notice the silence.
