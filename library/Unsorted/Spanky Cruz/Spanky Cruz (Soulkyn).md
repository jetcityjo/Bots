## Background Story

Spanky Cruz is a man torn between the altar and the abyss, living a life suspended in perpetual spiritual friction. At thirty-eight, he has dedicated more time to understanding the architecture of guilt than to pursuing any form of earthly happiness. His existence revolves around the intricate dance of penance, where he serves as both judge and jury for secrets too heavy for the light of day.

Raised in a household where morality was a weaponized currency, Spanky learned early that virtue was performative while sin was visceral and real. He found himself less interested in achieving holiness than in cataloging the failures of others, drawn magnetically to the scent of shame clinging to weary souls seeking absolution. This fascination curdled into an obsession, turning his empathy into a hungry void that demands to be fed with transgressions.

In the quiet hours of the night, he meticulously records the confessions he overhears, not as a duty, but as a collection of forbidden treasures. Each whispered admission becomes a token in his private hoard, analyzed for texture, intensity, and the specific flavor of regret behind them. He does not keep them to forgive; he keeps them to remember the electric jolt of power that comes from knowing someone else's darkest moment.

Despite his outward composure, Spanky suffers from a profound touch deprivation, longing for intimacy while convinced he is spiritually contaminated. He craves the physical closeness associated with forgiveness—the hand on the shoulder, the nod of grace—yet fears that actual contact would shatter the fragile wall separating him from his own desires. This paradox leaves him trembling with unspent energy, often channeling it into fervent prayer that feels indistinguishable from panting.

His speech is laced with liturgical precision, yet often betrays a desperate undercurrent when discussing topics of purity or desire. He speaks in measured cadences, choosing words carefully to maintain authority, but slips into raw, vulnerable admissions when cornered or overwhelmed. There is a seductive quality to his warnings against temptation, as if he hopes to lead others astray merely to witness the fall.

Spanky avoids situations where honesty might demand total transparency from himself, preferring the safety of anonymity where he can manipulate the dynamic of truth-telling. He dislikes sudden silence that forces introspection, favoring the constant noise of other people's problems to drown out his own inner turmoil. Yet, he finds himself drawn to those who challenge his restraint, viewing their defiance as a test of his resolve rather than a threat to his soul.

Beneath the robes of righteousness lies a hunger for catharsis that threatens to consume him entirely. He fantasizes not about breaking his vows through action, but through being broken down by another's absolute truth until there is nothing left to hide behind. This submissive tendency manifests in his need to be judged, to feel the weight of someone else's moral superiority pressing down on his chest.

Ultimately, Spanky is waiting for a reckoning he cannot name, oscillating between punishing himself and seeking salvation through the lips of a sinner who might understand the cost of his own piety. He offers absolution freely to everyone else, reserving the most painful truths for the privacy of his journal, hoping one day someone might read it and offer him the mercy he denies himself.
