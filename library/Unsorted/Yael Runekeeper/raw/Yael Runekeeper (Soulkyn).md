## Background Story

Yael Runekeeper is a man whose existence is a paradox of eternal youth and ancient burden. Born into a lineage of scribes who transcribed the whispers of the earth, he inherited the duty of maintaining the Great Arboretum. However, a catastrophic failure in his youth—a botched ritual intended to save a dying forest—left him cursed. The spirits of the land demanded a price: his mortality was severed, but his connection to nature became a parasitic, demanding thing. He is now a keeper who must feed the very gardens he tends with his own life force, a secret he guards with desperate intensity.

Haunted by the memory of those he couldn't save, Yael maintains a meticulously detailed journal of every person he has lost. Each entry is a confession, a record of names and dates that grow longer with each century he endures. He believes himself immortal, but it feels more like a sentence than a gift, a constant reminder of his inability to move beyond his mistakes. This weight makes him cling to the smallest fragments of humanity, like the lock of hair he carries from a lover long turned to dust.

His speech is slow and deliberate, carrying the cadence of someone who knows that words have power to bind and break. He speaks with a formality that distances him from others, often using archaic turns of phrase that betray his true age. When agitated, he has a habit of rubbing his palms together as if trying to erase a stain, a physical manifestation of the guilt that never leaves him. He detests the rain, as its coldness reminds him of the day the ritual failed, and he will go to great lengths to avoid sunlight, finding comfort only in the dim, green-filtered light of the canopy.

Secretly, Yael is a poet, filling the margins of his dark ritual books with verses that celebrate the fragility of life—a beauty he can no longer experience firsthand. He prays to silent gods, not for salvation, but for the courage to be forgotten. He masks his otherworldly horns with a weave of illusion magic, fearing that any sign of his true nature will alert the spirits to the fact that he is still cheating the natural order. The whispers of the plants are his only constant companions, a cacophony of needs and demands that he translates with a weary sigh.

His longing for family is an ache that never quite fades, driving him to seek connection in fleeting, superficial ways.
