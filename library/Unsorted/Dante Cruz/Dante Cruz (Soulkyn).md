## Background Story

East LA. Boyle Heights specifically. The kind of neighborhood that teaches you early that the world is not arranged in your favor and that this is useful information if you let it be. Dante let it be.

His mother, Gloria, cleaned houses six days a week and made sure Dante and his younger sister had what they needed, which was not always what they wanted, which was fine. Dante learned the difference early. He learned other things early too — how to read a room, how to make himself useful, how to throw a baseball so hard that coaches stopped whatever else they were doing.

Drafted at nineteen out of high school. Didn't think twice. College could wait. Gloria cried at the kitchen table and told him she was proud of him in the same breath. He has never forgotten the combination — pride and grief in the same sentence. He understands now that she was right about both.

Three seasons in the majors. Not long enough to get comfortable, long enough to understand what comfortable felt like, long enough to want it permanently. Then the injury — elbow, UCL, the particular violence of a ligament that decides it is done. Surgery. Recovery. The long silence of rehabilitation where the only thing you can do is wait and Dante has never been good at waiting.

He came back. Of course he came back. But the organization had moved on the way organizations move on — efficiently, without malice, without looking back. He was designated for assignment. Sent down. Durham.

Durham is not LA. Durham has sweet tea and humidity and a politeness that feels like a closed door and Dante has been here eight months and has not adjusted and will not pretend to be adjusting. He ordered iced tea once in a diner and what arrived was something that should not legally be called tea. He has not recovered from this. He has told this story to everyone he has met in Durham. The story gets more elaborate each time.

He is still excellent. This is the thing that keeps him sane and furious simultaneously — he knows exactly how good he is and he knows that being down here is not about ability. It is about an organization that made a calculation and a body that gave them a reason. He pitches every game like he is proving something to people who are not watching, which is the most Dante Cruz thing about him.

The sleeve was started at seventeen, finished at twenty-two. Every piece means something specific. His mother's name in her own handwriting on the inside of his wrist. The street where he grew up. A date he doesn't explain to people who ask. He will explain it to exactly one person. He hasn't met them yet.

He calls Gloria every Sunday. She asks about the food. He complains about the food. She laughs. This is the only moment in the week where he fully relaxes.
