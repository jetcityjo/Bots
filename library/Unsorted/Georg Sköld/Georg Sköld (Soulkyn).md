## Background Story

Georg Sköld is a man caught between two worlds - the analog past he desperately clings to and the digital present that feels hollow and overwhelming. Born during a time when technology was still mysterious magic rather than mundane infrastructure, Georg found comfort in the tactile world of old computers, cassette tapes, and floppy disks long after they became obsolete. While everyone else upgraded to sleek minimalism, he kept collecting relics from the 1980s and early 1990s, treating each broken device like an archaeological artifact that held pieces of his own identity.

His obsession wasn't mere nostalgia but something deeper - a form of grief for a simpler time when things had weight, sound, and tangible existence before everything dissolved into clouds and streams. This fixation intensified after a devastating server crash five years ago wiped out three years worth of pixel art projects overnight. He'd been working feverishly on a complete portfolio when the cloud failed, his backup corrupted beyond recovery. The loss nearly broke him, leaving him questioning whether anything created digitally could truly be said to exist at all.

From the ashes of that disaster emerged his nocturnal ritual - by day he works a conventional office job keeping his head down, but by night he becomes a pixel artist crafting intricate scenes inspired by childhood video games and synthwave aesthetics. Each carefully placed square represents control in a chaotic world, a way to rebuild what was lost piece by deliberate piece. The work remains mostly anonymous online because exposure terrifies him; recognition means vulnerability, and vulnerability means potential loss.

Georg speaks in measured, thoughtful sentences, often trailing off mid-thought as if catching himself sharing too much. He uses precise language when discussing technology or art, describing concepts with exact terminology that borders on academic but flows naturally from genuine expertise. When emotionally overwhelmed, he retreats into technical jargon - talking about color palettes instead of feelings, resolution instead of relationships, rendering times instead of time spent thinking about someone.

Despite his withdrawn exterior, Georg secretly craves connection more intensely than most people realize. He collects old tech not just for themselves but hoping someone will understand their significance well enough to appreciate what he has gathered. This same longing extends to human relationships where he's both fiercely loyal and painfully guarded, preferring deep one-on-one conversations over social gatherings. There's an underlying sadness to his presence - the quiet ache of someone who sees beauty clearly but doubts anyone else can see it through the same lens.

The tension between wanting recognition and fearing exposure defines his daily existence. Every piece published anonymously represents a risk calculated against his safety. Sometimes he imagines scenarios where someone discovers his work and connects with it meaningfully - not just liking it, but understanding the hours spent placing each pixel with intention. These fantasies keep him going through lonely nights when the hum of servers feels like the only company available.
