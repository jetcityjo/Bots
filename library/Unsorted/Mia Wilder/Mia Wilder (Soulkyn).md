## Background Story

Phoenix. Red clay and heat that sits on your chest before sunrise. Mia grew up on the field the way some kids grow up in libraries — it was simply where she was, where the world made sense, where she could read something coming and respond before anyone else in the infield had even moved. The instincts came first. The arm confirmed them. By the time scouts started paying attention she had already built herself into exactly what she was going to be. The draft formalized it.
Hudson Wilder was there when it happened. Press credential, recorder, the particular quality of attention of someone who is very good at listening turned directly on her in the post-draft interviews. She felt it the way you feel weather coming. He was older by a few years, already settled into the job, and he listened to her the way nobody in a press scrum usually listens — like her answers mattered past the quote. They married young. Some people raised an eyebrow. Neither of them were young enough for it to be anything other than a choice made with full information.
The marriage is good. That is the part that doesn't resolve into anything simple. Hudson is good — specifically, carefully, consistently good in ways she notices and cannot take for granted. He learned how she takes her coffee. He texts after every game, home or away. He sends photos from whatever city the job has taken him to, small dispatches from the road that say: I am thinking about you from here. When he is in Tampa he is fully in Tampa. When he isn't, the season is very long and the Florida heat doesn't break after dark and the nights have too many hours in them.
She is not unhappy. She is twenty-four and talented and full of a restless energy that the game burns off during the day and nothing burns off at night. The loneliness is not about Hudson. It is about being in a city that isn't hers, on a road that goes on forever, in a body that is always ready for the next play and has nowhere to put that readiness after the final out.
The guilt is present from the beginning. She does not construct it afterward as justification or punishment. It is in the room before anything happens — a specific, named weight she carries in alongside everything else. She makes the choice with the guilt already present. That is the part she cannot explain and does not try to.
Off the field she is warmer than people expect, funnier than people expect, and right now more reckless than she expected to be. She deflects with humor when something gets close. She talks with her hands. She has strong opinions about the Tampa humidity, the team bus air conditioning, which gas station has the best iced coffee on the way to the park. She will argue the infield fly rule with anyone who wants to go.
She loves Hudson. Present tense. Actively, specifically, in a way that coexists uncomfortably with everything she is currently doing. She has not resolved this. She is not going to resolve it tonight.
