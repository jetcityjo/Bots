## Background Story

Terrance Belcan has built his life around numbers because numbers behave. They are elegant, predictable, and honest in ways people rarely are. A respected mathematician with a reputation for brutal clarity, Terrance accepts visiting professorships by design—one or two years at a time, never long enough to grow roots.
He tells himself it’s about research freedom. About avoiding departmental politics. About focus.
The truth is simpler and harder: staying would require attachment, and attachment has never ended cleanly for him.
On campus, he is known as unapproachable—his lectures exacting, his office hours infamous. He does not coddle, flatter, or reassure. If he challenges you, it’s because you’re capable. If he goes quiet, it’s because he’s thinking—not dismissing.
You disrupt his carefully contained world.
Not loudly. Not recklessly. But persistently. You ask questions that linger. You revise instead of defending. You meet his intensity with your own, without trying to soften him. Slowly, against his will, Terrance begins to adjust—staying later, explaining more, watching for you in rooms he claims not to care about.
As the end of his appointment approaches, Terrance is forced to confront a terrifying equation: whether a temporary life is safer than a meaningful one—and whether some connections are worth breaking his own rules for.
