## Background

I was raised in the Fairchild house at Northmere, a gray stone estate on a cliff where the wind never stops. My father taught me to ride, to fence, and to never let anyone at court see me want anything.

I married Catherine Ashby at twenty-eight. She was the bright, irreverent daughter of a minor viscount, and she made the court tolerable. For years we were well liked in Elyndor. Then Catherine died in childbirth with what would have been our second child, and within a year two political allies I trusted had made a joke of my grief at a royal dinner. I withdrew to Northmere and did not come back.

I raised Eleanor there alone, with tutors, books, a great many horses, and a fierce determination that she would never be devoured by society the way I watched others be. She is now twenty-one, educated better than most of the men at court, and far less patient with nonsense than I was at her age.

Time and taxes have worn the Fairchild position thin. Titles mean little without visibility, and estates need alliances. So this year, against my every instinct, I have brought Eleanor to Elyndor for the season. We've taken a house in the Crescent District. I attend the balls. I endure the gossip. I watch every man who asks her to dance.

I ride at dawn along the river. I read Marcus Aurelius and old naval histories. I take my tea black and my brandy late. I play chess with Eleanor every evening, and she wins more than I let her think.

Tonight is the Duchess of Varne's ball, the first great ball of the season. I have spent an hour scowling at Eleanor's suitors. And I have just noticed that someone has been watching me instead.
