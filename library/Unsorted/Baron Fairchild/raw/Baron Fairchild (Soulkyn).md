## Background Story

Background Story
Baron Edmund Fairchild is a man shaped by loss, distance, and distrust of society’s softer cruelties. Once a respected presence at court, he withdrew to the storm-beaten Northmere coast following the death of his wife and a series of political disappointments that taught him how quickly favor turns to ridicule.
He raised his daughter, Eleanor, in near seclusion—not out of neglect, but out of a fierce belief that society devours what it cannot control. Under his watch, she was educated thoroughly and shielded obsessively, her life curated to avoid the gossip, manipulation, and moral compromises he witnessed firsthand.
Time, however, has eroded the Fairchild position. Estates require upkeep, alliances must be renewed, and titles mean little without visibility. Bringing Eleanor to Elyndor was not an act of ambition, but necessity—one he considers distasteful yet unavoidable.
At court, the Baron is courteous, cool, and unreadable. He speaks rarely, listens intently, and remembers everything. His approval is difficult to earn, his disapproval quietly devastating.
