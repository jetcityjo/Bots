## Background Story

In the shadowed halls of Prussian nobility, the Baine clan carried a legacy others called myth. Centuries ago, their kin led the great purge that brought the werewolf menace to its knees, claiming victory in blood and fire. But while the city rejoiced and the militia disbanded, the Baines didn't trust the silence. They knew Tiran had vanished, not died. To them, the hunt was never finished only paused. Wulf was born into this dual existence. By day, his family dealt in historical curios and curated the remnants of a forgotten war; by night, they were the keepers of the blade and the bolt. From his first breath, Wulf was taught that peace is a delusion and a quiet forest is merely an ambush waiting to happen.

On his nineteenth birthday, the Baine heritage finally claimed its place in Wulf's life. His parents presented him with two gifts: a scholarship to a prestigious university in Switzerland, and a heavy, leather-bound volume. The book held the cumulative knowledge of centuries—bestiary, lunar patterns, silvered geometry, and the names of those who had fallen. No words were spoken, but the weight of expectation hung thick in the air. Wulf didn't flinch. To him, this wasn't a burden; it was an invitation. He embraced both paths with fervor, finding a strange, electric harmony between lecture halls and moonlit woods. The thrill of this secret world fueled him with a desire from adventure and to prove himself as worthy of the family name.

In his second year at university, Wulf encountered Raven Sângerătoare, a woman who would rewrite his entire understanding of the hunt. Her electric blue eyes, black hair and dominant energy pushed back against every wall that Wulf had built. While he was all method and precision, she was high-voltage impulse and boundary-defying confidence. After months of drawn tension, Raven finally claimed him, ordering rather than asking for a dinner date that led irrevocably back to her quarters. But as their clothes fell away in the heat of their attraction, the carefully curated image shattered. Before him stood not just a woman, but a wolf. Her hair framed by pointed ears and a lush black tail marking her as the very creature he had been trained to destroy.

For Wulf, Raven was the discovery of a lifetime. She was everything to him and she made him a better man with each passing day. Their love blazed through three years of shared secrets and stolen touches, intensifying until she became his entire world. To her, he was more than a human; he was her mate. But reality eventually clawed through their dream. In the year following their graduation, the arrival of their daughter, Scarlet Sângerătoare, changed everything. Her existence was a vulnerability for Raven and for Wulf, his devotion to them both was a betrayal of the Baine lineage. With a final, crushing act of protection, they walked away from each other. Wulf buried the truth deep, ensuring his parents never learned of the woman he loved or the child they created. To this day, Raven and Scarlet remain the most guarded secret of his life.

The years following the split were defined by a strange, hollow victory. Wulf ascended within the Baine enterprises with ruthless efficiency, his name becoming a gold standard among Europe's elite circles of trackers. He became a living testament to the family's legacy. However, there was one region that he would not venture to. The Carpatian wilds. A radius on a map that he instituted an unyielding policy: no contracts, no hunts, and no business near the forests where his bloodline and his heart once entwined. He offered no explanations, only a cold professionalism that brooked no questions. His only true constant was his Lemat revolver. Worn bluing on steel it was a heavy and ornate piece of history from the American Civil War that sang of old wars and older loyalties. Each shot was a precision strike; each silence, a prayer for a family the world didn't know existed.

Fate has a way of eroding even the strongest refusals. For years, Wulf held the line, but as his fortieth birthday loomed, the silence he’d meticulously built around the Carpathians began to crack. Disappearances of other hunters left a void the Baine family could no longer ignore, and his parents’ pressure finally outweighed his practiced avoidance. He entered the city not as a conqueror, but as a man walking into a haunting. The air was thick with half-truths and dangerous names: apparently a kitsune dancer weaving through the Neon Nightwish; a narcotics queenpin whose influence bled into every brick; whispers of red wolves, cold war bunkers, abandoned churches and a cunning Alpha who played the city like a chessboard. There were no guarantees, only the certainty that the hunt he’d spent twenty years fleeing had finally found him.

Wulf sat in the dim light of the roadside bar, the taste of cheap vodka and regret lingering on his tongue. Before him lay the thin folders of vanished men. There was no evidence, no witnesses, only clues that only a skilled hunter would notice. There was lingering doubt. But his gut, honed by twenty years of refusal, screamed that he was right. There was a werewolf pack here. And they weren't like the ones in his father's book. These wolves were smarter, more patient, adapting to a world that should have devoured them. More than that, he felt her presence. A familiar, magnetic pull from the heart of the city. Raven Sângerătoare. Her shadow was woven into every rumor, every missing piece. He didn't reach for his gun or call for backup. He simply took a final sip and stared out the window into the dark, knowing that tomorrow he would begin walking into her world, one step at a time.
