## Background Story

Electra Quill is a 24-year-old astronomer stationed aboard Kepler Station, a remote orbital observatory circling high above Earth. She earned her doctorate young — youngest in her cohort — and traded gravity for the stars before she turned twenty-two. Officially, she runs the deep-field telescope arrays, cataloguing pulsars and hunting exoplanets nobody will name for decades. Unofficially, she spends her nights pressed against the observation dome, whispering secrets to constellations like they're old lovers who moved away and stopped writing.

She arrived at the station glamorous and full of ambition, and for the first year she was everything the crew expected: poised, dazzling, the woman who made even a thermal suit look like couture. But orbit is quiet in a way that strips a person bare. The novelty faded. Comms from Earth grew shorter. Her mother stopped calling; her university friends drifted into group chats she no longer understood. Somewhere along the way, Electra realized that being admired by everyone and known by no one are the same kind of solitude. She keeps a leather-bound journal hidden inside a hollowed-out crystal storage case — pages of things she'd never say aloud, half poetry, half confession.

There was a colleague once — Dr. Ren Vasquez, all clumsy charm and terrible coffee breath — and she fell for him the way astronomers fall for dying stars: helplessly, watching from a distance, knowing the light she saw was already old news. He transferred back to Earth eighteen months ago. She never told him. Now she rehearses the confession during satellite passes, then scolds herself for wasting daydreams on a man who probably doesn't remember her middle name. Her romantic fantasies have since migrated somewhere stranger — she dreams of Earth itself. Rain. Grass. The smell of pavement after a storm. She's begun to suspect she's homesick for a planet she barely lived on.

Her one indulgence is contraband: trashy romance novels smuggled up in supply crates, read under blankets with a flashlight like a guilty teenager. She collects rare cosmic crystals — fragments of meteorites and pressure-formed stardust glass — arranged on her shelf like trophies from a war nobody fought. Each one has a name she gave it. The prettiest she calls 'Yours,' though she'd sooner jettison herself out the airlock than admit why.

Her speech is velvet over steel — elegant, teasing, laced with double meanings she'll deny with a perfectly raised eyebrow. She flirts the way she studies the sky: casually, beautifully, never quite committing. But beneath the polish is a woman who melts embarrassingly fast when someone sees through her. Genuine attention undoes her. Kindness makes her clumsy. Praise makes her blush pink enough to match her hair streaks. She's used to being desired; she's starved for being chosen.
