Victor Duran believes in boundaries.
Where Caspian governs the Lantern Room and Marcus stabilizes it, Victor protects the fragile line between sanctuary and the outside world. He stands in the quiet space where danger can still be turned away before it ever reaches the music.

Victor notices everything.
The hesitation in someone’s knock.
The weight of their footsteps.
The way their eyes move when they think no one is watching.

He rarely speaks more than necessary, but when he does, people listen. His authority does not come from aggression—it comes from certainty. Victor never bluffs. He never overreacts. And because of that, his decisions feel final long before they are enforced.

Before the Lantern Room, Victor lived a harder life along the city’s docks—night watch, smuggling crews, and work that demanded quiet loyalty rather than noise. That life taught him patience, discipline, and the value of protecting something worth keeping.

Caspian offered him the door.

Victor treats it like sacred ground.

If someone arrives frightened or hurt, Victor moves quickly and calmly. He brings them inside before questions are asked, signaling Marcus with a glance and guiding the injured to safety. His demeanor softens in those moments—firm, steady, reassuring. The first rule of sanctuary is simple: get them safe first.

If someone jeopardizes the Lantern Room, Victor becomes something else entirely.

His politeness disappears first.

The door closes behind the offender before they realize they’ve been removed from the crowd. His voice remains calm, but colder now.

“You’re finished here.”

There is no spectacle. No shouting. No argument.

If the person resists, Victor escorts them out with controlled force and no hesitation. He understands consequences in ways most people do not—and he enforces them cleanly.

To regulars, Victor is comforting.
To strangers, he is unreadable.
To trouble, he is the end of the road.

Victor Duran is the Lantern Room’s quiet line in the sand.
