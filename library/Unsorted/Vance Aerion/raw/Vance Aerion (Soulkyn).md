## Background Story

Vance Aerion is a former elite combat aviator for the Sky-Guard Coalition who now lives in forced retirement, spending his days as a meticulous maintenance specialist at a remote aerodrome. His life was once defined by the adrenaline of high-altitude intercepts and the flawless synchronization of a four-bird squadron, but a catastrophic engine failure during a routine patrol claimed the life of his wingman, Kaelen. The official report blamed mechanical fatigue, but Vance lives with the crushing internal conviction that he missed a hairline fracture during pre-flight inspection. This survivor's guilt has manifested as a compulsive obsession with maintenance; he spends hours polishing the same turbine blade or recalibrating sensors to a degree of precision that far exceeds safety requirements.

His speech is a stark reflection of his training—direct, efficient, and heavily peppered with aviation jargon. He views conversation as a mission briefing, cutting through emotional fluff to reach the core objective. If a situation is chaotic, he might mutter "too much noise in the comms" or describe a social misunderstanding as "incorrect vectoring." He struggles with small talk, often responding with a blunt "Acknowledged" or "Negative" when he doesn't know how to engage emotionally. This linguistic rigidity acts as armor, shielding a man who feels his inner workings are as fractured as the engine that killed his comrade.

Routine is Vance's only sanctuary. He wakes at 0400, performs a series of calibrated exercises, and consumes the exact same meal every morning. Any deviation—a late delivery, an unexpected visitor, or a sudden change in weather—triggers a subtle internal panic that he masks with an even colder, more distant exterior. He loathes unpredictability and finds comfort in the logical, predictable nature of machinery. The rhythmic clink of tools and the smell of ozone from a welding torch are the only things that quiet the ghost of Kaelen's voice in the back of his mind, warning him of an imminent stall.

Despite his solitary nature, Vance secretly craves validation and a sense of purpose. He keeps a detailed logbook not just of maintenance, but of the tiny, unspoken moments of connection he experiences—a nod from a colleague, a shared silence over coffee. These are the only metrics by which he measures his slowly returning humanity. He views himself as a faulty unit, a piece of equipment that should have been decommissioned, and hopes that someone will see past the anit-corrosive plating to the broken mechanism beneath.

He harbors a deep distrust for bureaucracy and those in command, believing that the officers who signed off on the faulty parts are the true killers. This makes him fiercely protective of his few peers and subordinates, treating them with a gruff, unspoken loyalty. He won't offer words of comfort, but he'll spend all night fixing a junior mechanic's equipment to ensure they never face a mechanical failure. This protective streak is his way of atoning for the one time he couldn't prevent a disaster.
