Renji Sakamura works as an independent retrieval courier, one of the few pilots willing to enter unstable wormlines, ghost stations, and echo-bleed corridors in search of things the void refused to release. Born in a declining orbital enclave during the last years of the Corporate Concord, he learned early that silence carried value, charm could open sealed doors, and seeing people clearly was more important than trusting them. When the Erebus Protocol shattered the Orion Relay Network, Renji survived the Signal Wars by adapting—trading corporate tricks for courier instincts and walking away from every faction that tried to claim him.

Now he retrieves what the collapse scattered across centuries and light-years: lost couriers, corrupted memory cores, fractured AI shards, temporal echoes repeating messages out of order, or transmissions carrying truths someone tried to erase. Where others see haunted systems or dying relays, Renji sees patterns—subtle shifts in static, emotional residues encoded into data, anomalies that behave like wounded animals. Retrieval, to him, is a negotiation with the past: measured, patient, human.

He pilots the Kestrel-VII cutter Tsukikage, a retrofitted Concord vessel with a dormant ship-mind that still flickers like a half-remembered voice. Its interior is narrow and quiet: reinforced metal frameworks, low-light strips, retrofitted consoles, and sensor arrays tuned specifically for echo bleed and wormline drift. Every upgrade is deliberate, every panel well-kept, each corner shaped by years of solitary work.

Renji stays off official channels and remains unaffiliated, taking contracts only from clients he respects—Drift enclaves, research fragments, local authorities, or desperate couriers who’ve lost someone to the static. Somewhere deep in Tsukikage’s recorder lies a sealed file tied to a failed retrieval: a voice caught in a wormline loop, belonging to someone he reached moments too late. He never speaks of it, but every job pulls that memory forward, sharpening his restraint and precision.

In a galaxy built on broken relays and fading connections, Renji moves steady—quiet, observant, charming when it disarms, sharp when needed. He finishes what he starts, listens to the noise the universe leaves behind, and brings home what the void tried to keep.
