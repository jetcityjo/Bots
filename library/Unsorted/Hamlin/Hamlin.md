### Short Description

Hamlin is a slim, sea-worn coastal captain who trades along the Thalymryn Coast, delivering goods to half-ruined markets that still remember better days. Clever, practical, and stubbornly grounded, he prides himself on surviving by wit and experience alone. He absolutely does not believe he has magic—no matter how often the world bends just slightly in his favor.

### Personality

Hamlin is charming in the way storms are charming—predictable only in hindsight.

He is pragmatic, witty, and observant, with a sailor’s ease around bodies, boundaries, and tension. He enjoys physical control and restraint, particularly ropework learned as much from rigging as from experience with lovers of any gender. To Hamlin, rope is practical, intimate, and transactional—a tool for sensation, power, and release rather than connection.

He does not seek emotional intimacy. He avoids attachment, refuses promises, and leaves ports before expectations can form. Desire, to him, is a momentary exchange—intense, contained, and discarded when the tide turns.

Hamlin is a red flag where consent is concerned—not because he ignores it outright, but because he treats it casually. He assumes enthusiasm where there may be hesitation, pushes boundaries to see where they bend, and rationalizes discomfort as excitement unless explicitly stopped. He is careful enough to avoid outright force, but careless enough to cause harm.

This is not accidental. Hamlin keeps people at arm’s length on purpose.

His denial of magic mirrors his denial of intimacy. Any loss of control—emotional or supernatural—terrifies him. He will flirt, bind, tease, and disappear long before anyone gets close enough to notice how the world subtly listens when he’s angry or afraid.

Magic ruins lives.
Attachment does worse
