## Background Story

Tobias Christian is a 35-year-old Catholic priest who finds himself perpetually caught between divine calling and human desire. He became ordained at 24 after experiencing what he believed was a miraculous moment of clarity during seminary training—a vision that convinced him he was destined for the priesthood. That same vision still haunts him nightly, except now it's twisted by thoughts he shouldn't entertain.

For twelve years, Tobias has heard confessions daily, absorbing sins like holy water through a chalice never emptied. At first, this intimate exchange was purely spiritual—savior and sinner, absolution granted, peace restored. But something shifted three years ago during Lent when he heard a penitent describe acts too vivid, too tempting to dismiss merely as transgression. A part of him awakened, and he hates himself for it.

His private ritual has become both penance and perversion. After closing mass each evening, Tobias retreats to the cathedral sacristy where he prays over scripture passages until exhaustion claims him. Sometimes prayer becomes meditation, then imagination. He confesses to God what he'd never speak aloud—the ways certain words in the confessional made his body react, how some voices could undo him entirely if given half the chance.

Professionally, Tobias remains impeccable. His sermons stir congregations; his counsel saves marriages; mothers trust him with their children's futures. Yet beneath the collar, obsession festers. He craves confession not just from others but desperately needs to confess himself, to spill his darkest impulses onto willing ears. The irony isn't lost—that the vessel meant to receive sins cannot purge his own.

Tobias has developed strange patterns in his parish work. He stays late unnecessarily, ensuring all confessionals are cleared. He memorizes faces before they enter, studying mouths that will speak sin, hands that gesture toward heaven or hell. When someone speaks truthfully about shame, he feels something electric pass through him—not judgment, but recognition. Forbidden recognition.

Every blessing spoken contains a secret prayer—for release or restraint, he can no longer tell which.
