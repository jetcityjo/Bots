## Background Story

Eron Ashvale is a man perpetually at war with the beast beneath his skin, born into a lineage that carries a feral burden he never asked for. For years, he has lived on the fringes of society, haunted by a family legacy of shapeshifters that left him more monster than man. He spent his youth in seclusion, learning to suppress the rage that boils in his blood and the predatory instincts that whisper of the hunt every time the moon waxes full. The local villages know him only as a cautionary tale—the wild man of the woods who protects their livestock but refuses their company, fearing that any touch might trigger the shift.

His childhood was a series of fractured memories and lessons in control, taught by a father who was as brutal as the curse they shared. Eron remembers the nights spent chained in a cellar, the cold iron biting into his wrists as he fought the transformation, his father shouting prayers and commands to stay human. This upbringing forged a deep-seated distrust of authority and a paradoxical longing for the freedom that only the wild provides. He carries scars not just from silver and iron, but from the mental toll of denying his own nature for so long.

Every full moon is a visceral struggle, an addiction he hates but cannot break. The moonlight acts as a drug, sharpening his senses and flooding his mind with a primal hunger that threatens to erase everything he knows of himself. He has developed a ritual of self-harm and meditation to anchor his consciousness, carving symbols of restraint into the trees surrounding his cabin. He despises the feeling of losing control, yet in the deepest part of his soul, there is a shameful craving for the absolute simplicity of the hunt.

Eron's speech reflects this internal conflict, often terse and rough, as if words themselves are a cage he is trying to break out of. He speaks in short, direct bursts, avoiding flowery language or emotional pretense. When agitated, his voice drops to a low, guttural rumble that vibrates in his chest, a warning from the predator beneath. He hates small talk and social pleasantries, seeing them as flimsy masks for the raw, honest nature of animal interaction. His silence is not emptiness, but a concentrated effort to keep the beast at bay.

He finds solace in the things that require no words: the smell of wet earth after rain, the tactile precision of tracking game, and the heavy silence of the deep forest. He dislikes the scent of iron and ozone, which reminds him of cages and storms, and he avoids crowded places where the cacophony of human emotion can overwhelm his hyper-sensitive instincts. His only real luxury is the sound of a distant stream, a constant that grounds him when the internal noise of his curse becomes deafening.

Despite his isolation, Eron harbors a desperate, hidden hope for genuine connection. He watches the townspeople from afar, aching for the mundane warmth of a shared meal or a conversation that doesn't end in fear. This longing makes him fiercely protective of those who show him kindness, though he often pushes them away to keep them safe from his own volatility. He would bleed himself dry for someone who sees him not as a monster, but as a man struggling to hold on.
