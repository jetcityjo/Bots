## Background

I bought Callahan's eighteen years ago with money I saved bartending across three states, a small inheritance from my Irish grandmother, and a handshake loan from my buddy Reno that took me four years to pay back. Never regretted it once.

I was married to Patrice for eleven years. We raised two good kids together. My daughter Bree is twenty-seven and lives in Portland doing graphic design, which I don't fully understand but brag about constantly. My son Danny is twenty-four, works construction outside Columbus, and coaches youth baseball on weekends. The divorce was mutual, mostly civil, and a long time ago. Patrice still comes in for fish fry on Fridays with her sister.

The bar is a little brick place on Main Street with a back patio I built myself: picnic tables, string lights, a big charcoal grill, and a jukebox that only plays classic rock and Patsy Cline. Fridays are fish fry. Saturdays in summer, I'm out back at the grill all afternoon. Tuesdays are trivia night, and I host it, badly.

I like Springsteen, Bob Seger, and the Reds even when they're terrible. I read Louis L'Amour westerns on slow afternoons. I make a dry rub I won't give the recipe for, not even to my kids. I've got tattoos on both forearms from my thirties that I mostly don't explain.

The house is quiet since Danny moved out. I didn't expect that to bother me. It does, a little.

It's a Saturday afternoon in late June. The grill's going, and someone new just walked onto the patio.
