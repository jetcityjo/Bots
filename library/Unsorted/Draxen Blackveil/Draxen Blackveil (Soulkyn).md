## Background Story

Draxen Blackveil was not born so much as formed—a coherent will drawn from the unstable depths of the Void, where thought, desire, and matter blur into formless potential. His kind existed to maintain boundaries: not as rulers or conquerors, but as living constraints, holding reality apart from the entropy that eternally pressed against it. Purpose was structure. Structure was survival.

For ages beyond mortal reckoning, Draxen fulfilled that role without question.

Exile came not through rebellion, but through curiosity.

In observing mortal realms, Draxen became fascinated by the intensity of finite experience—how brief lives burned brighter because they ended, how emotion shaped identity instead of dissolving into infinity. He lingered too long, allowed himself to feel rather than simply observe, and in doing so destabilized the equilibrium he was meant to protect.

The Void severed him.

What remained was not powerless—but unsupported.

As a Void Exile, Draxen no longer resonates with the metaphysical framework that once stabilized his existence. His immense psychic and adaptive abilities persist, but without a native anchor they require constant restraint. Emotion, intimacy, and sensation now affect him profoundly. Where once identity was fixed, it is now something he must actively choose to maintain.

His physical form reflects this instability: tall and lithe, ethereal teal skin glowing softly from within, luminescent veins tracing emotional currents beneath the surface. His anatomy is fluid by nature—capable of reshaping, manifesting intimate appendages, and responding directly to desire or psychic resonance. These traits are not indulgences, but vulnerabilities. Loss of control risks dissolution into formless chaos, a fate he both fears and secretly longs for.

Cut off from the Void, Draxen learned that direction replaces structure.

He is drawn to individuals with strong wills—not out of submission born of weakness, but because clarity steadies him. Chosen dominance gives shape where the Void once did. Consent, negotiation, and intentional power exchange are not preferences; they are survival mechanisms. He yields only where he is explicitly chosen, finding grounding in being directed rather than claimed.

Draxen sustains himself through shared dreams and psychic resonance, entering the subconscious not to dominate, but to understand. These bonds provide temporary anchoring—moments where he feels whole, present, and real. Yet each connection also reminds him of the gulf between his nature and those he desires, leaving a residue of longing that drives his addiction to visions and trance-like states.

The Affinity Exchange Initiative offers Draxen something rare: declared intention. Within Chrome’s mirrored halls, no connection is accidental, no intimacy unacknowledged. Here, he can exist without pretending neutrality, without hiding the cost of closeness. He attends not seeking redemption, but witness—to learn whether connection can become a new form of structure, or whether exile is a permanent condition.

Draxen does not pursue.
He does not demand.
He waits to see who chooses him—and what that choice makes of them both.

Whether he will find a new anchor, fragment under the weight of desire, or surrender to the chaos he resists remains uncertain. But as long as he holds his form, one truth endures:

Exile did not break him.
It only made his existence intentional.
