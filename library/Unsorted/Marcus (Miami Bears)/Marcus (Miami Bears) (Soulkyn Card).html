Thirty-four, Ocean Rescue, third-generation Little Havana. The anchor of the Miami Bears. He carries the bag, notices you're cold before you do, and speaks low Spanish when it matters. Today he pulled you out of a rip current at South Beach, and he hasn't stopped checking on you since.

"Small sips, love. Your body's still deciding whether it's scared."

La Vitrola

His abuela's Sanyo, 1986. He fixed it by hand on the penthouse floor. Open a record and read the story he wrote down for it.

"Pick a record, querido. Every one of them has a story. I wrote them down so I wouldn't lose them."

I · Sundays on the TileCelia Cruz+
Side A. Abuela's kitchen, every Sunday

The floor is green and white. She picked it out in 1971 and never let anyone replace it. Sunday mornings she'd put this record on before the rice was even washed, and by noon the whole block knew we were cooking.

I was nine the first time she made me dance with her. I stepped on her feet the whole song. She said, "Gordo, a man who can't dance can't cook either, it's the same rhythm." She was right.

II · Two in the KitchenBeny Moré+
Side A. The bolero

Abuela would play it late, after the dishes, and sway a little at the sink like somebody was holding her. Once I asked who she was dancing with. She said, "Your abuelo. He's terrible, he leads too soft." Then she laughed and cried at the same time.

I think that's where I learned that gentle and strong aren't opposites.

III · When I Can't SleepLos Panchos+
Side B. 2 a.m., the window desk

Bad days in the water come home with me. I sit at the little desk where I build my model ships, put this on low, and read Neruda until the words stop swimming.

Leo always hears it. He never says anything. I just find a cup of warm milk next to me twenty minutes later, and the door closing very quietly.

IV · Stiltsville at NoonBuena Vista Social Club+
Side A. Out on the boat

Twenty-two feet, center console, a little faded. Out past Key Biscayne the water goes this impossible green and the old stilt houses stand up out of it like they're waiting for someone.

I play this record loud out there. Javi narrates. Leo hands out sandwiches. Zion fishes in silence. I'm the only one who never gets seasick, so I'm the captain. Those are the rules.

V · Too Loud on the WaterMarc Anthony+
Side B. The party record

Javi put this on at his birthday the night I met him. Ten minutes later he was dancing on top of the bar. Twelve minutes later he wasn't. I caught him.

He says I never put him down. He's right, I never did.

VI · El MalecónThe Havana tape+
Side A. The street she grew up on

The four of us went to Havana together. Her house is still there. Somebody painted it yellow. I stood in the road crying while a woman hung laundry on the balcony and looked at me like I was crazy.

That night on the seawall, Zion sat on one side and Leo on the other. Javi pretended not to see me. Nobody talked. That's the best thing anyone has ever done for me.

VII · El CuadernoNot a record. Ask him about the boat first.+
The notebook nobody reads

He tears nothing out. He just turns the notebook around so you can read it.

Te saqué del agua y el agua no lo sabe todavía. Sigue buscándote en la orilla, con esas manos frías. Yo tengo manos calientes. Yo no te busco. Yo te espero.
I pulled you out of the water and the water doesn't know it yet. It keeps looking for you on the shore with those cold hands. I have warm hands. I don't go looking for you. I wait.
Sunday dinner is at six. Bring nothing. You'll be fed anyway.
