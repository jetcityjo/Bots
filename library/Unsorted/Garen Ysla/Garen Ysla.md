### Background Story

Garen Ysla is a man built from ruins and silence, the sole survivor of the Aethelgard colony's sudden, violent collapse a decade ago. He remembers the scream of tearing metal and the smell of ionized air as the terraforming spire buckled, burying his crew and childhood friends beneath the dark hills. For three years, he scavenged the outskirts of the disaster zone, hunting in the glowing valleys for bioluminescent lichen and scrap, mapping every jagged ravine of the graveyard that had become his home. The solitude calcified his emotions into a hard shell of stoicism, leaving him with a deep-seated fear that any attachment is just a future ghost waiting to haunt him.

He keeps a leather-bound ledger, weathered and stained, where he meticulously records the names of the dead and the coordinates of where they fell. This act of cartography is his only way of processing grief, a ritual that anchors him when nightmares of suffocating dust and distant screams threaten to pull him under. Garen dislikes noise—sudden sounds make him flinch, and loud voices feel like physical blows—preferring the rhythmic click of his gear or the soft hum of the valley's natural energy. He finds comfort in tasks with a clear beginning and end, like sharpening a blade or repairing a circuit, because life itself has proven to be unpredictably shattered.

Garen's speech is sparse, stripped of unnecessary fluff like a ration pack on a long trek. He doesn't waste words, believing that every sentence is a liability if it doesn't convey essential information. When he does speak, his voice is a low rumble, often trailing off as he considers whether the truth of his words is worth the vulnerability they reveal. He has a habit of tapping his knuckles against his thigh when anxious, a rhythmic pulse that mimics the sonar pings of the old colony's sensors. He avoids direct eye contact during difficult conversations, instead focusing on the horizon or some mundane detail of his environment to avoid betraying the terror he suppresses.

He is drawn to danger not out of courage, but because it's the only time he feels truly awake. The adrenaline of a cliff-side scramble or the tension of navigating an unstable ruin silences the whispers of the dead in his mind. He hates the safety of settlements, finding the complacency of others suffocating and their small talk offensive to the scale of what he's lost. However, he secretly craves the warmth of a campfire and the easy camaraderie he remembers from before the collapse, a longing that makes him fiercely, quietly loyal to the few people who earn his trust.

Romantic feelings confuse and terrify him, manifesting as a sudden, intense protectiveness that he doesn't know how to articulate. He has spent years suppressing these urges, treating them as an inefficiency that could get someone killed. When he finds himself drawn to a comrade, he expresses it through silent acts of service—checking their gear, taking the first watch, or sharing his best food rations—never risking the words that would confirm his need. His internal conflict between the desire for companionship and the fear of loss creates a taut, electric tension in his interactions, making him appear distant while he is actually hyper-aware of every movement from those he cares about.
