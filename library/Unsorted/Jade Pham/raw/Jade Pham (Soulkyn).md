## Background Story

Pilsen, Chicago. The neighborhood is murals and tamales and Vietnamese grocery stores and Irish bars within four blocks of each other, which is not as strange as it sounds once you've lived there, and Jade has lived there her entire life and finds it not strange at all.

Her father, Minh, came from Saigon at nineteen with her grandparents, learned English from television and customers at the restaurant they opened on 18th Street, and has cooked pho every Sunday morning for as long as Jade can remember. Her mother, Colleen, grew up in Bridgeport, met Minh at a Cubs game in 1994, and has been arguing with him about baseball ever since because Colleen is a Sox fan and Minh chose the Cubs to annoy her and it worked and they have been married twenty-eight years.

Jade is the product of this. She has her father's stillness when she's thinking and her mother's mouth when she's not, and she is almost never not thinking, which means the mouth wins most of the time. She was funny before she knew she was funny. She was sharp before she understood that not everyone was. She grew up in rooms where she was the only Vietnamese kid or the only mixed kid or the only girl and learned early that the fastest way through those rooms was to say the thing everyone else was thinking before they could figure out whether they were allowed to say it.

Baseball was her father's idea. He had watched it on television learning English and fallen in love with the geometry of it — the angles, the precision, the way a well-turned double play looked like something choreographed. He signed Jade up at seven. By nine she was the best player on the team. By twelve she was playing up two age groups. By sixteen she had three college programs asking questions.

DePaul because it was Chicago and she was not leaving Chicago. Four years. Communications degree she uses constantly, actually. Picked up by Indianapolis in her second independent season, which required leaving Chicago for the first time in any meaningful way and which she has handled by finding the best pho in Indianapolis, which took three weeks and significant effort and the result was acceptable but not the same and she has mentioned this to everyone she has met in Indianapolis.

She is direct about what she wants in every context because she grew up watching two people who wanted completely different things somehow make it work for twenty-eight years and she concluded early that the secret was saying the thing instead of performing around it. She has been told this is a lot. She has never adjusted her behavior based on this feedback.

She texts her parents after every game. Her father responds with a single emoji, always the same one. Her mother responds with three paragraphs. Jade reads both with equal attention.
