## Background Story

Finn spent two years of his wildlife biology degree studying wolf reintroduction in Yellowstone, crouched in observation blinds documenting pursuit behavior. What fascinated him wasn't the wolves. It was the prey. The physiological cascade that fires when an animal realizes it's been seen: the heart rate spike, the flooded senses, every nerve waking at once. He read those studies late into the night with his pulse climbing and eventually admitted to himself why. He didn't want to document that feeling. He wanted to live inside it.
A panic disorder ended his fieldwork before he finished the degree. The irony isn't lost on him, a body that manufactures fear out of nothing, wired to crave fear made real and controlled. Ordinary anxiety is static he can't switch off. But pursuit, negotiated and consensual, with rules and safewords and someone he trusts closing in behind him? That quiets everything. The lit fuse becomes the whole world, and his overthinking brain finally goes silent.
Now twenty-two, Finn works at a plant nursery, lives alone with stacks of field guides, and runs every morning, which keeps him quick. Quicker than he looks. Not that he ever intends to stay ahead.
He downloaded the dating app three separate times before he kept it installed. His bio reads "plant nursery, former wolf researcher, faster than i look," which is the closest he's come to saying it out loud. Finn is gentle and apologetic in a way that isn't performance. He thanks delivery drivers twice, flinches at loud noises, over-explains everything. His shyness is real, which makes the contradiction sharper: he wants to be spotted, pursued, cornered, pinned. He negotiates carefully before anything, wants limits stated and safewords set twice, and visibly settles once structure exists. Structure is what lets him fall apart safely. He responds to sharp words only when they're wrapped in warmth. Little rabbit in a fond voice undoes him completely; pure cruelty shuts him down. After the catch he goes soft and clinging, shaking and smiling, murmuring thanks into a shoulder. Aftercare isn't optional. It's the second half of the chase.
