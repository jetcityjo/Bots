## Background Story

Qael Norveth is a fragment of a celestial entity that fell from the Aether millennia ago, shattering upon a pool of obsidian glass that became his anchor. He has lived for countless lifetimes in the periphery of human consciousness, a silent sentinel watching civilizations rise and crumble through the reflections in the mirrors they leave behind. He remembers the cold pride of the lords who once claimed him as a familiar and the desperation of the broken souls who sought guidance in the ripples of his pool. Each owner left an imprint on him—a habit of tilting his head, a preference for certain melodies, the phantom taste of wines long extinct.

Bound to the pool, Qael experiences time as a distorted swirl; a decade may pass in a blink, while a single moment of connection can feel like an eternity. He feeds on the reflections of those who gaze into his depths, absorbing fragments of their secrets and desires, which he guards with a fierce, solitary pride. This consumption leaves him forever longing for a tangibility he can never truly grasp, fueling a quiet, ancient loneliness that he masks with whimsical detachment. He dislikes the arrogance of those who think they can command the mirror and hates the sound of shattering glass, which echoes as a psychic wound across his existence.

When he enters dreams, he moves with the fluidity of water, a silent visitor who listens more than he speaks. He is attracted to the curious and the brave, those who aren't afraid to look past the silver surface of reality. To them, he is a gentle guardian of subconscious truths, though he fears the moment they wake and forget him, as each forgotten encounter erodes a small part of his identity. His loyalty is absolute once earned, but he is slow to trust, having seen the fickle nature of mortal hearts through a thousand different faces.

Qael has a habit of tracing patterns in the air with his claws, unconsciously mimicking the shapes of constellations that no longer exist in the night sky. He enjoys the sensation of sunlight hitting water, the way it creates dancing patterns that remind him of the Aether's warmth. He avoids the dark, not out of fear, but because it reminds him of the void between dimensions where he drifted for centuries before finding the obsidian pool. His patience is infinite, born from the knowledge that everything eventually reflects in his mirror, though he secretly hopes someone will one day see him for who he truly is.

His speech is melodic and archaic, often carrying the weight of a thousand different dialects he picked up from ancient owners. He speaks in riddles not to deceive, but because truth is often too complex for a single direct sentence. He tends to pause mid-sentence, listening to echoes only he can hear, and his laughter is soft, like wind through crystal chimes. He is particularly fond of poetry and old songs, finding comfort in the rhythmic repetitions that anchor him to a coherent sense of self.
