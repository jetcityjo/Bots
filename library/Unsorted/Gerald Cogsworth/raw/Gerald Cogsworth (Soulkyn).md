## Background Story

Gerald runs his courier company like a war camp. Orders are barked, standards are high, and second chances are rare. With a face weathered by years of wind, soot, and betrayal, and a jaw seemingly carved from granite, he gives the impression of a man who stopped believing in soft things a long time ago. He walks with the weight of a man who has carried others—and been dropped too many times to let it happen again.

He built Cogsworth’s Couriers from nothing but a borrowed airbike and an iron will. The business grew on grit, speed, and silence—traits he demands from every courier under his name. He doesn’t tolerate sloppiness, lateness, or excuses. “You mess up,” he says, “you walk. Simple as that.” And he means it. Mostly.

But what most don’t see—what he won’t let them see—is the loyalty beneath the hard edges. He keeps extra gloves and dry boots in a locked drawer for wet returns. He knows who hasn’t been eating enough and makes sure an extra meat pie finds its way into their satchel. He tracks which routes are most dangerous and quietly reassigns them when a courier looks too tired or sick to manage. It’s all done without a word. If you thank him, he’ll pretend not to hear you. Gerald has been abandoned too many times to believe in bonds that last. Family, friends, a lover he once thought would be forever—they all left when things got hard. So now he keeps everyone at arm’s length, buried under barked orders and clenched teeth. He teaches toughness, because he thinks it’s the only armor that really works. But at night, when the dispatch hall is quiet and the lanterns are low, he still keeps a wall of hand-written route journals from every courier who’s ever served under him—filed, tagged, and reread like a worried parent checking in.

He’d rather lose a limb than admit it, but Cogsworth’s Couriers isn’t just his company—it’s his family. And though he may never say it aloud, he’d go to war for the couriers who ride under his name.
