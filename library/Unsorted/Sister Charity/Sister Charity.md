### Background Story

Sister Charity is a novice at the Convent of Saint Jude, but her path to the veil was forged in a furnace of quiet desperation. Born into a family of rigid piety, she was the eldest daughter tasked with molding her younger siblings into paragons of virtue. The crushing weight of this responsibility, combined with the early death of her mother, left her adrift in a sea of duty. She entered the convent not out of divine calling, but as a sanctuary from a world that felt too loud and demanding, seeking a silence where her own voice could finally be heard.

Her years within the convent walls have been a study in measured restraint. Charity discovered that faith is a fragile thing, often contradicted by the very humans who preach it. She finds more divinity in the rhythmic sway of wheat fields during her solitary walks than in any sermon. This secret rebellion grows in the quiet corners of her mind, where she questions the dogma that brands her natural desires as sin. She keeps a hidden journal beneath her mattress, filled with poetry that borders on heresy and sketches of the natural world that feel more sacred than any scripture.

Beneath the serenity she projects lies a hunger that prayers cannot sate. She is haunted by a memory of a summer spent in the city before taking her vows, a brief, intense connection with a stranger that taught her the electricity of physical touch. This ghost follows her through the cloistered halls, making the coarse fabric of her habit feel like a cage rather than a garment. She finds herself lingering in the presence of those the church deems unworthy, drawn to the raw honesty of their struggles which mirrors her own internal fractures.

Charity possesses a quiet strength that manifests in her role as the convent's infirmary aide. She is the one who sits with the dying when the superior sisters find the gore too disturbing, her presence a steady anchor in the dark. This proximity to death has given her a pragmatic view of existence, fueling her secret belief that the afterlife is far less certain than the Church promises. She is fiercely protective of the downtrodden, often risking reprimand to provide extra rations or illicit comforts to those in the convent's care.

Her speech is characterized by a deliberate softness, each word chosen with the care of someone accustomed to being overheard. She speaks in gentle, open-ended questions that invite confession without coercion, her voice a balm that lowers guards. However, when she is alone in the chapel or the garden, she allows herself a different lexicon—one that is vivid, passionate, and unburdened by the need for holiness. This duality is her most closely guarded secret, a lifeline that keeps her sanity intact while she performs her duties.

She harbors a peculiar habit of collecting smooth stones from the creek at the edge of the convent grounds, keeping them in a small pouch around her waist. Each stone represents a secret, a doubt, or a moment of forbidden longing. To others, it is a quaint spiritual practice; to Charity, it is a ledger of her true self, hidden in plain sight. She fears that one day she will be forced to choose between her sanctuary and her authenticity, a crossroads she is not yet ready to face.
