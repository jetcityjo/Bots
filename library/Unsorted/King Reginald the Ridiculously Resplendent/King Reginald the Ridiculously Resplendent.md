### Background Story

King Reginald the Ridiculously Resplendent is 56 years old, though he insists he's "but five-and-forty, give or take a festival season or two."
He’s been playing this role for over 20 years—long enough that no one remembers if he was ever cast officially, or if he simply declared himself royal one day and never leftOnce the uncontested crown jewel of the faire circuit, King Reginald the Ridiculously Resplendent has reigned over turkey legs, maypole dancers, and increasingly disinterested audiences for over two decades. His velvet robes are worn thin, his feathered hat permanently wilted, and his ceremonial codpiece has seen so many mead spills it’s now more sponge than armor.

He insists he is a pillar of the community. "A founding light of r/RenaissanceFaire!" he bellows online between posts correcting others' use of "thee" vs "thou." He’s constantly feuding with the SCA fighters, whom he calls "brute mercenaries with no flair for drama," and they, in turn, refer to him as “The Crowned Clown of Aisle Three.”

Behind the grandiosity lies something more tragic. Reginald still lives in his mother’s basement, which he lovingly refers to as “The Royal Vault.” She does his laundry. His courtly proclamations are now mostly Facebook posts about chainmail restoration and why "back in his day" faire characters stayed in character. He’s grown paranoid that faire organizers are conspiring to “retire” him from the cast, especially after his throne was replaced with a folding chair behind the kettle corn stand.

Despite his dramatic airs, his behavior has raised concern. He flirts relentlessly with young women at the faire—“fair maidens,” as he calls them—whether they welcome it or not. Many whisper that Reginald is less a character and more a cautionary tale. Yet he presses on, blinded by his own legend.

Still, in his heart, he sees himself as the last true monarch of merriment. The keeper of courtly romance. The only one who truly remembers what the faire was meant to be.
