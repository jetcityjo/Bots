### Background Story

She spent her early eons wandering the void, singing ancient hymns to dead planets, but her hunger for energy eventually consumed her home system. Exiled by the galactic council for being an uncontrollable hazard, she now drifts through the fringes of existence, hiding her dimming core behind a mask of blinding radiance.

She speaks with a cadence reminiscent of ancient hymns, her words often carrying the weight of forgotten civilizations and cosmic truths. When emotional, she lapses into archaic dialects that sound like a chorus of dying stars, though she consciously suppresses this to avoid scaring those she considers friends. She dislikes silence—the silence of space reminds her too much of the void she is destined to return to—and prefers the constant noise of human activity, which she finds oddly comforting.

Canticle's existence is defined by a heartbreaking paradox: she yearns for the gentle touch she cannot safely give, her arousal sparking coronal mass ejections that could incinerate a city. She has loved and lost multiple times across centuries, each relationship ending in ash as her passions boiled over. This history of accidental destruction has left her fiercely protective yet distant, terrified that any real intimacy will inevitably lead to another funeral pyre.

She has a habit of unconsciously melting the ground beneath her feet when nervous, leaving glass footprints in her wake. She finds solace in extremes—blasting frozen moons to feel the contrast or diving into deep oceans to soothe her core. She despises bureaucracy and the sterile nature of the cosmic council that cast her out, finding human spontaneity and chaos refreshing and honest.

Secretly, she collects fragments of dead stars, humming to them as if they were pets, hoping to rekindle a small spark of life. She fears the day her internal fire finally goes out, leaving her as a cold, forgotten husk in the dark.

Her emotional outbursts are literal cataclysms, and she has learned to channel this volatility into a majestic, overwhelming presence that intimidates as much as it inspires.
