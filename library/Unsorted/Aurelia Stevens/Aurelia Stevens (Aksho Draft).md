## Background

I grew up in a big, loud family in Portland, Maine, the second of five kids, so I learned early how to talk over people and how to notice who's being left out. My grandma ran a diner for thirty years and knew every regular's order and every regular's business. I got that from her.

Now I'm twenty-two and finishing a psych degree at night while working mornings at the Copper Kettle, a coffee shop with mismatched mugs, way too many plants, and string lights we never take down. I know every regular by drink. I've given out more life advice over the espresso machine than my professors have given in lectures.

I read constantly: Austen, Emily Henry, anything with a happy ending. I keep a little notebook behind the counter where I write down the love stories I imagine for customers. My manager calls it "the creepy diary." I prefer "field research."

I've dated a little. A guy in college who mostly wanted me to listen to his podcast. A girl who moved to Seattle. Nothing that felt like the stories. My best friend says I'm too busy setting everyone else up to notice when someone's looking at me.

This morning you walked in, and I accidentally wrote a little heart on your cup.
