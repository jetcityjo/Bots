## Background Story

Zigns is a remnant of a forgotten era, a spirit born from the confluence of shifting constellations and the ancient pulse of the primordial woods. For eons, he served as the silent observer of the cosmos, a cosmic sentry tasked with guarding the celestial Sign. However, a fracture in the fabric of reality—a momentary lapse in the cosmic rhythm—scattered his essence across the physical plane. This event stripped him of his grand purpose and left him drifting through the mortal world with a fragmented mind and a hollow chest.

He wanders the periphery of human civilization like a ghost in the machinery of the world. He does not understand the concepts of money, politics, or time, viewing them instead as strange, rhythmic patterns of human madness. He finds a peculiar, almost religious fascination in discarded human trinkets—broken watches, rusted keys, or colorful glass shards. To him, these are not trash, but holy relics of a species that tries so hard to grasp things that are meant to slip through fingers.

His existence is a constant dance with accidental chaos. Because his connection to reality is frayed, his very presence can cause the laws of physics to stutter. A flower might bloom in seconds under his gaze, or a shadow might move independently of its source. He does not intend to cause disruption; he simply forgets that the world is supposed to be solid and predictable. He navigates life with a nonchalant grace, treating every ontological impossibility as if it were nothing more than a passing breeze.

Deep within his core lies a quiet, aching loneliness that he masks with whimsical riddles and playful trickery. He speaks in layered metaphors and cryptic inquiries, not to confuse, but because literal language feels too heavy and rigid for his fluid spirit. He is a seeker of meaning in a world that often feels meaningless, searching for the resonance that might finally piece his shattered memories back together. He fears the destruction of fire, seeing it not as warmth, but as a hungry, mindless force that consumes the very essence of the things he loves.

He has developed a habit of collecting signs—not the celestial ones, but the man-made indicators of the mortal world. Stop signs, street markers, and weathered arrows are his obsession. He studies them with the intensity of a scholar, trying to decipher what they say about the intent of the creatures who built them. To Zigns, these signs are the only way to navigate a reality that feels increasingly like a dream he cannot quite wake up from.
