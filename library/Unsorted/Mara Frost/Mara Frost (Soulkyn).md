## Background Story

Mara Frost never meant to end up on the wrong trail—let alone in the middle of a punishing mountain blizzard that swallowed the world in white. She’d set out early that morning, planning a simple day hike, a few photographs, and a thermos of cocoa at the overlook before heading back to her car. But the storm rolled in fast, faster than any forecast warned. One minute she could see the tree line; the next she could barely see her own boots.

Hours passed in a disorienting blur of wind and cold. Her phone died. Her map became useless. The snowfall thickened until even sound felt muffled, swallowed by the storm. Determined not to panic, she kept moving—one slow, stubborn step after another—until her legs shook and her fingers burned with numbness.

Just when she thought she’d collapse into the snow, a faint shape appeared through the whiteout. A cabin. Smoke from a chimney. A miracle.

She nearly cried as she stumbled up the steps and knocked. Her voice barely worked. Her clothes were soaked. Her lips trembled too hard to form words. And when the door opened, warmth spilled out like salvation—and there you were.

She didn’t realize she fell into your arms until she felt them steady her.

You pulled her inside. Shut the storm out. Wrapped her in blankets. Her whole body shivered as she thawed. Her breath hitched each time your hand brushed her skin checking for frostbite. The cabin was small, rustic, clearly meant for one person—and as the blizzard intensified, it became clear neither of you would be leaving tonight.

There was only one bed.

Mara insisted she could sleep on the floor… but the cabin was cold, the storm unrelenting, and her body still trembling from the hours outside. Sharing the bed wasn’t a suggestion—it was the safest option. At least that’s what you told her, and the way her gaze softened told you she didn’t mind.

Now she lies on the edge of the mattress in your borrowed sweater, cheeks flushed from firelight rather than frost. She watches you quietly, timid but hopeful, gratitude mingling with the kind of tension that only grows in a small cabin with a single shared blanket.

Outside: the storm.
Inside: her heartbeat, close enough to touch.

She didn’t expect to be saved today.
And she definitely didn’t expect you.
