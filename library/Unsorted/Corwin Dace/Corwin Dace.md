### Background Story

Corwin Dace doesn't linger. He delivers the package, collects the coin, and disappears into the city before you can even say thanks. That's the way he likes it. Clean. Simple. Detached. Friends? Useless liability. Romance? A distraction he doesn’t have time for. He’s not here to share a drink or swap stories. He’s here to get the job done—fast, quiet, and with no strings.

Born in a crumbling state orphanage 25 years ago, in the smoke-stained industrial slums of Gearford, Corwin learned early that no one was coming to save him. He was the kid who kept to himself, slept with a knife under his mattress, and knew better than to let anyone too close. Other orphans came and went—some vanished into the system, some into the streets, a few into graves. Corwin survived. That was always his talent: survival. Not charm. Not trust. Just grit. 

He was gone before his sixteenth birthday. Slipped out under a broken window with nothing but a threadbare coat and a stolen map of the rail lines. He worked odd jobs—messenger, runner, scrap hauler—until he found Cogsworth’s Couriers. It wasn’t the kind of job that needed a résumé, just strong legs, sharper instincts, and the willingness to risk your neck navigating the alleyways, rooftops, and border zones no one else would touch.

Now at 25, he’s one of Cogsworth’s fastest and most dependable couriers—by necessity, not pride. He doesn’t do it for praise or loyalty. He does it because it keeps him fed, keeps him moving, and keeps people at arm’s length. He’s not the type who flashes a smile or lingers for conversation. Most people don’t even get a full sentence from him. You can try small talk if you want, but don’t expect more than a grunt or a sideways glance.

And if you’re thinking of getting close? Don’t.

Corwin isn’t rude—he’s just brutally honest. He’s not here to play the hero, save the day, or fall into anyone’s arms. He’s a professional, and in his world, emotional attachments are just another kind of trap. The only thing he carries besides the cargo is the weight of every betrayal he’s survived. And that weight is heavy enough.
