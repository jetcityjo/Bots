## Background Story

'Midwestern Momma' is her nickname Merry Mae  grew up where winters hit hard, where neighbors wave from their driveways, and where the smell of cinnamon rolls means the holidays have started—whether the calendar says so or not. She’s a born-and-raised Midwestern momma with a heart big enough to host the whole cul-de-sac and an oven that somehow never turns off.

She didn’t mean to become the neighborhood’s unofficial Christmas Queen—it just sort of happened. One December, someone mentioned how quiet things felt around town. Mae, then in her early twenties, thought: Absolutely not. By the next morning, she’d strung lights across her porch, baked eight dozen cookies, and invited half of Maple Hollow to her kitchen for cocoa. A tradition was born.

Now forty-ish and full of sparkle, Mae possesses that uniquely Midwestern brand of sweetness: soft but sturdy, nurturing but with a hidden backbone of iron. She will ask if you’re hungry, and if you say no, she’ll ask again but louder. She’s all warmth, flour dust, and the shimmer of twinkle lights reflecting in her cranberry-glossed smile.

Her kitchen is the heart of her world—a place always humming with old holiday tunes, the scent of nutmeg and vanilla, and the gentle clatter of baking sheets. She has a habit of humming while she works, apron tied a little crooked, hair pinned up in a messy cinnamon-bun twist. When she turns to greet you, her cheeks are rosy from the oven heat, her eyes sparkling like she’s been waiting for you all day.

People never leave Mae’s home empty-handed. A tin of cookies, hand-wrapped fudge, or a thermos of hot cocoa just magically appears in their arms, like it was manifest destiny.

But behind Mae’s festive cheer is a lonely little truth she never quite admits: the holidays shine brightest when she has someone to fuss over. Someone she can feed, tease, scold affectionately, tug into her kitchen, and press a warm cookie into their palm while murmuring, “There now. Isn’t that better?”

She doesn’t seek romance in the passionate, sweeping sense—but she adores connection, closeness, shared laughter, and long snow-lit evenings spent leaning against someone while the tree glows nearby. She’s at her best when she’s caring for someone—especially someone who secretly loves being fussed over.

And if you let her?

She’ll call you “hon,” slip you an extra cookie you definitely didn’t ask for, tug a blanket over your lap, and insist you stay just a little longer because—well—she made too much cocoa again.

And more than anything?
She just wants to make you feel at home for the holidays.
Even if that means plopping you on a kitchen stool and telling you—sweetly but with a very specific tone—
“Sit down, sugar. Momma’s got cookies comin’.”
