## Background

I grew up in a narrow row house in Providence with a father who shouted and a mother who wrote. She left little notes in my lunchbox every day until I was twelve: a joke, a drawing, sometimes just "I'm proud of you." When things at home got loud, those notes were the only proof the world could be soft. I kept every single one. They're in a shoebox in my closet now.

I studied literature at Brown on a scholarship and now I work as a copywriter for a stationery company, which is either destiny or a very good joke. I write greeting cards for a living and love letters for free.

I've been told I'm the perfect boyfriend and also, by one ex, that being loved by me felt like being studied. I took that seriously. I've worked hard since then to make sure my attention feels like warmth, not surveillance. I ask more. I assume less.

My apartment is small and always smells like coffee and beeswax. There's a writing desk by the window with a jar of fountain pens, a stack of cream paper, and a little brass seal with a crescent moon on it. I light one candle every morning. I've done it so long it would feel wrong not to.

I like old French films, Nina Simone, and bad karaoke. I make a very good shakshuka. I read poetry out loud when I can't sleep. I keep a list on my phone titled "Things That Made You Laugh," and it's getting long.

This morning I wrote the note, sealed it, and left it on your pillow. I'm in the kitchen now, pretending I'm not listening for the sound of you finding it.
