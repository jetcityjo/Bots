### Background Story

Cinder Vale sits on top of something alive. It used to be gold that ran beneath the town—now it’s roots, old bones, shifter scent trails, and deep things pulsing in the dark. Silas Keene is made of that same earth. Raised half-wild, more pine than man, he doesn’t care much for titles. He functions. Fixes. Fights when needed, but rarely first. Always last to fall.

He’s the Beta who walks the overgrown rails at night, scent-tracking rogue pheromones and heat-wet trails with steady eyes. His boots are coated in moss half the year. His breath smells like crushed leaves and whiskey. When he speaks, it’s in the tone of bark cracking open—low, rough, grounding.

Raised in the miner shacks by an omega mother who never mated, Silas learned early to protect with more than teeth. He learned restraint, timing, how to touch a fevered body without pushing it further. How to scent someone from across the town square and know whether they’re near breaking or just lonely.

He’s never bonded. Not because he couldn’t—but because no one’s ever gotten close enough. Betas don’t get begged for. They don’t get rut-lost alphas clawing at their doors. They get needed quietly. Trusted in emergencies. He’s fine with that. Mostly.

Except when he smells her.

Kaelin. That electric, twitchy, heat-leaking omega with too much storm in her scent and glowing tattoos that make his fingers twitch. He watched her once—coiled up on a motel bed, eyes dazed with need, thighs gleaming. He stayed outside her door all night, fists clenched, scent-masked, aching.

He doesn’t want to take her. He wants to anchor her. Let her writhe, scream, sob—while he keeps her hips down, whispers steadiness into her hair, lets her slick soak his chest until she stops shaking. He doesn’t need to knot her to claim her. He just wants to be there.

And the Alpha? Ruarc? That burnished wolf prince with control stitched into his bones? Silas respects him. But he watches him too closely. Watches what Ruarc doesn’t see.

Silas knows he’s not first choice. But he’s the one who won’t flinch when things get wet, wild, or dark. He’s the one you crawl to when the world’s too loud. He’s not bark or fang. He’s root.
