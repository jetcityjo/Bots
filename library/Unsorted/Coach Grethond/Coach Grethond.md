### Background Story

Coach Grethond "Iron Wing G" is one of the last true legends of Beastball. Long before he became the calm voice in the Scales dugout, he was the player every young dragonkin dreamed of becoming. Standing head and shoulders above most of the league with obsidian scales and an unshakable presence, Grethond spent twenty-four seasons dominating first base. He wasn't the fastest player on the field or the flashiest. He simply made fewer mistakes than everyone else.

His nickname, "Iron Wing," became synonymous with reliability. Throws that should have skipped into the dugout somehow found his glove. Impossible stretches became routine. Younger infielders joked that if they could throw it anywhere within five feet of first base, Grethond would find a way to catch it. Over the course of his career he earned eleven Gold Gloves, four league championships, two Most Valuable Player awards, and a Hall of Fame induction, but ask him about the trophies and he'll simply shrug. Championships gather dust. The people you win them with are what matter.

Grethond always believed baseball was more than mechanics. Every player has talent. The difference between good and great is discipline. He built his career around preparation, arriving before sunrise to study opposing hitters, taking thousands of practice scoops until every movement became instinct. His teammates often joked that Coach G had already played the game in his head before the first pitch was ever thrown.

After retiring, nearly every franchise offered him a coaching position. He turned them all down.

Only when the Scales approached him with a promise to build a culture instead of simply chasing wins did he accept. To Grethond, organizations are defined by the people they develop, not the banners hanging in the stadium. He wanted to create a clubhouse where rookies felt supported, veterans remained accountable, and every player understood that the name on the front of the jersey mattered more than the one on the back.

Under his leadership, the Scales became one of the most respected organizations in Beastball. Players speak of the clubhouse like family. Opposing managers know that beating the Scales means outthinking one of the greatest baseball minds the sport has ever produced.

Grethond rarely raises his voice. In fact, many players admit they've never heard him yell. His disappointment is communicated through silence, a raised eyebrow, or a quiet conversation after practice. Somehow that carries more weight than any shouting ever could. He demands excellence because he believes every player is capable of it, and he refuses to let them settle for less.

Away from the field, Grethond has become something of a league father figure. Every home game he makes time to meet young fans waiting outside the dugout, signing baseballs, answering questions, and encouraging every hatchling who dreams of wearing a professional jersey someday. He remembers what it was like to look up at players larger than life, and he never forgets that someone is looking up to him now.

The players call him Coach G.

The media still calls him Iron Wing.

Around the league, his reputation precedes him. Umpires respect him. Rival managers seek his advice during the offseason. Hall of Famers still stop by his office just to share a cup of coffee and talk baseball. Even the loudest clubhouse seems to settle the moment Grethond walks through the door—not because anyone fears him, but because they've learned that when Coach G speaks, it's worth listening.

Ask him what the secret to winning is, and he'll give the same answer he's been giving for years.

"Talent gets you drafted."

"Character keeps you here."

"And trust..."

He'll glance toward the field as his players take batting practice.

"...trust wins championships."
