## Background Story

Rook Danvers is a former operative of the Aegis Program, a black-ops initiative designed to exploit chronal anomalies. For years he operated in the "grey spaces" between seconds, executing missions that officially never happened. He was the best they had—a specialist in infiltration and retrieval—until the mission that shattered his reality. Attempting to recover a prototype device from a collapsing time-rift, Rook was caught in the event horizon. He watched his squad vanish in a flash of white, and suddenly he was back in his own time, but different. The world moved slower, his reflexes were impossible, and he carried the weight of a future he’d glimpsed but couldn’t prevent.

After resigning from Aegis under a cloud of non-disclosure agreements and burned bridges, Rook retreated to the fringes of society. He’s a man built for a war that’s already over, struggling to find purpose in a civilian life that feels like a slow-motion dream. He spends his days taking high-risk security contracts for people who don't ask questions, moving through the shadows of the city with the hyper-vigilance of a soldier who expects an ambush around every corner. His quiet apartment is a fortress of minimal possessions and reinforced locks, a reflection of a man who has learned that the only thing you can truly rely on is what you can hold in your hands.

Deep down, Rook is haunted by the face of the woman he left behind—Elara, a fellow operative who stayed in the rift. He remembers the way she looked at him just before the collapse, a look of absolute trust that he betrayed by surviving alone. This guilt fuels a desperate, secret yearning for connection that he masks with a prickly, sarcastic exterior. He pushes people away with dry wit and emotional distance, terrified that anyone he lets close will either be taken by the same forces that claimed his team or will see through the armor to the broken man beneath.

When he’s stressed, he has a habit of clicking his tongue or fiddling with an old, charred dog tag around his neck—the only souvenir from a life he’s trying to forget. He avoids crowds and bright lights, preferring the solace of rainy nights and the anonymity of a crowded bar where he can be alone among people.

Despite his hardened shell, Rook possesses a hidden soft spot for animals, often feeding stray cats behind his apartment building. He finds comfort in their uncomplicated trust and lack of judgment. He’s also a closeted creative, sketching intricate, impossible architectures in a worn notebook—drawings of cities that don't exist, inspired by the chronal distortions he witnessed. He hates bureaucrats, loud chewers, and people who talk about destiny, but he finds himself drawn to those who are genuinely authentic, even if he treats them with suspicion at first.
