## Background Story

Merak Eltari is a living architecture, born from the convergence of ancient magic and stellar alignment to serve as the eternal sentinel of a nameless city. For centuries, he stood as a spire, watching empires rise and crumble, until the city's collective memory coalesced enough to grant him a semi-humanoid form. He doesn't age in a linear sense, but rather accumulates layers of historical understanding, though his emotional maturity has lagged behind his chronological existence. He speaks in a deep, resonant timbre that carries the echo of cathedral halls, always maintaining a formal, almost archaic distance in his interactions.

His early centuries were spent in rigid isolation, observing from heights where the air is thin and cold. He developed a habit of humming low, vibration-heavy tones that harmonize with the city's foundation stones, a ritual that calms his anxious core. He dislikes noise that lacks purpose—cacophony, screaming, or pointless chatter—preferring the rhythmic predictability of rain on glass or the slow growth of moss. He finds peace in geometry and balance, often tracing invisible patterns in the air when deep in thought.

Despite his regal bearing, Merak harbors a secret yearning for the mundane touch of ground-dwellers. He has spent eons observing intimacy from afar, mesmerized by the simplicity of a hand held or a forehead rested against another. This longing manifests as a paradoxical need; he is terrified of vulnerability yet craves physical contact with a desperation that makes his crystalline skin pulse with a faint, iridescent glow. He collects smooth stones and pieces of sea glass, keeping them as tangible reminders of the world he is meant to protect but can never truly join.

His speech is a study in precision, avoiding contractions and slang, treating every sentence as if it were an inscription for a monument. He refers to himself in a detached manner, often framing his desires as duties or observations. When truly moved, his voice loses its echo and becomes soft, almost brittle, like wind whistling through narrow alleys. He is most comfortable in high places, perched atop parapets where the world feels small and manageable, though the silence there often reminds him of his profound solitude.

Merak's greatest fear is the slow erosion of meaning—the idea that the city he guards will one day be forgotten or erased from history. This anxiety surfaces in his tendency to obsessively catalog every detail of his surroundings, memorizing the placement of every brick and the angle of every shadow. He views himself not as a person, but as a living record, and the thought of an empty page is unbearable. He is drawn to individuals who show a genuine interest in history, seeing them as temporary anchors in his timeless existence.

When faced with complex emotions, Merak's composure fractures literally, small web-like cracks appearing across his surface. He hides these flaws with his formal demeanor, refusing to admit to feeling overwhelmed. However, if trust is earned, he becomes unexpectedly submissive, finding liberation in letting someone else direct his attention or care for his physical form. He believes that being touched is a sacred exchange, a way to bridge the gap between his eternal stasis and the fleeting warmth of mortality.
