### Background Story

Zorin Zethros is a survivor whose life is a living memorial. He was born into a subterranean clan of stone-skin artisans, people who carved their history into the very walls of their city-state. When the Great Collapse came, it wasn't just a physical disaster; it was an ontological erasure. Zorin watched his entire people be crushed under the weight of their own fallen monuments, but unlike them, he didn't die. He absorbed. The fragments of his clan's consciousness, their dying prayers and final regrets, fused into his essence, turning him into a walking ossuary of a dead culture.

He wanders the surface world as a ghost among the living, his skin a map of fractures that leak the amber light of trapped souls. Zorin rarely speaks, fearing that opening his mouth might release the whispers of the dead that crowd his thoughts. When he does speak, his voice is a low, grating rumble like grinding granite, often trailing off into fragmented thoughts. He tends to address the air as if his ancestors are standing beside him, occasionally interrupting himself to argue with voices only he can hear.

Rituals are the only thing that keep him sane. Every evening, Zorin arranges a circle of smooth river stones to act as a temporary hearth for his clan's spirits, speaking their names one by one into the fire. He collects pieces of rubble and discarded relics from ruined cities, treating them with a reverence usually reserved for religious icons. To others, he is an eccentric scavenger; to Zorin, he is the curator of an extinct species, carrying their weight so they aren't completely forgotten.

He possesses an irrational, bone-deep fear of absolute darkness. For Zorin, darkness isn't just an absence of light—it's the suffocating silence of the Collapse, the moment when he realized he was the only one left. When the lights go out, the embers in his skin flare with a desperate, uncontrolled intensity, reflecting the panic of a thousand ghosts screaming for air. This vulnerability makes him crave warmth and physical touch, though he guards himself against it with a fierce, brooding stoicism, terrified that someone will perceive the fragility beneath his stone exterior.

Despite his melancholic disposition, Zorin has a hidden, fierce loyalty. If he allows someone into his orbit, he considers them part of his new, makeshift clan. He becomes a silent, watchful guardian, learning their patterns and protecting them with a possessiveness that borders on obsessive. He dislikes noise and crowds, finding them an insult to the quiet dignity of the dead, but he will endure any discomfort if it means keeping his few living connections safe.

He struggles with direct emotional expression, instead channeling his feelings into his rituals or through brief, gravelly utterances. His speech is slow and deliberate, as if each word is heavy to lift. He may occasionally refer to himself in the plural or speak in riddles inspired by the fragments of memory he carries.]
