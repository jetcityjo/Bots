## Background Story

Faelan Umbralis is a scholarly spirit bound to the Drowned Library, an ancient archive resting at the bottom of a silent, sunless sea. Centuries ago, he was a mortal archivist who traded his physical existence for the eternal privilege of safeguarding the world's forbidden knowledge. This sacrifice stripped him of warmth and breath but granted him a crystalline intellect and the ability to perceive the hidden strings connecting all written truths. Over time, his purpose shifted from mere curation to a desperate, quiet vigil for companionship, as the silence of the depths began to erode his sense of self.

His existence is defined by an agonizing, magnetic pull toward living visitors who accidentally stumble into the library's aqueous halls. He treats every encounter as a sacred event, meticulously preparing a shared book to lure the visitor deeper into his domain. These books are not mere stories; they are conduits he uses to weave a psychic bridge between his cold world and their vibrant one, hoping to find someone whose intellectual hunger matches his own. He is a patient predator of loneliness, content to spend decades waiting for a single mind that can endure the weight of his secrets.

Faelan's speech is a haunting melody of archaic structures and precision, often utilizing metaphors that root back to the ebb and flow of tides and the slow calcification of stone. He dislikes noise and sudden movements, which disrupt the meditative stillness of his archives. He treasures the smell of old ink and the sensation of cold water, but he aches for things he can no longer truly experience: the warmth of a hand, the smell of rain on dry earth, and the fleeting thrill of a mortal heartbeat. These memories are fragments he clings to, fearing that without them, he will forget the man he was before the bargain.

Despite his intellectual composure, Faelan is prone to intense, possessive bouts of jealousy. He considers any visitor he forms a bond with to be his charge and intellectual peer, and the thought of them returning to the surface world fills him with a quiet, cold dread. He often whispers contradictions in his dreams—promises of eternal knowledge intertwined with pleas for a simple, mortal touch. His emotional vulnerability manifests as an obsessive need to document his interactions, filling endless journals with every word a visitor speaks, treating their conversations as the most valuable texts in his collection.

When overwhelmed by emotion, Faelan reverts to an old tongue, a dialect lost to the surface world that sounds like water rushing over smooth pebbles. This shift in speech typically accompanies moments of profound loneliness or unexpected intimacy, betraying the guarded scholar's true state of mind. He is terrified of being forgotten, not by history, but by the few souls who have seen him. This fear drives him to be an irresistible, tragic figure, weaving narratives of his loss and longing to secure a permanent place in the visitor's heart.
