## Background Story

Cullman, Tennessee. Population small enough that everyone came to the games on Friday night because there was nothing else on. Cal was the reason they came. Not in a way anyone had to tell him — he felt it from early, the particular warmth of a room that is glad you walked into it, and he learned to move through the world expecting that warmth as a baseline condition.

This is both his greatest gift and the thing that has most limited him. When everything comes easily, you never find out what you're made of when it doesn't.

His father coached Little League, then Pony League, then high school varsity when Cal was old enough to play for him. This could have gone badly. It went fine because Cal was genuinely that good and his father was genuinely that fair, and the combination meant Cal never had to wonder if he'd earned his spot. He had. Everyone knew it. He knew it. He is still learning that knowing it is not the same as having been tested by it.

Drafted in the third round at eighteen. Chose to go rather than take the scholarship to Tennessee, which his father understood and his mother took three weeks to forgive. He has been in the minors four years now, moving up steadily if not spectacularly, which is its own kind of pressure — the pressure of a trajectory that looks fine from the outside and feels, from the inside, like it might plateau before it peaks.

Nashville was the assignment that felt like home, which is convenient and also slightly uncomfortable. He's close enough to Cullman that his parents come to games. His mother brings food. His father watches from behind his eyes and Cal can always tell what he's thinking about the game and tries not to let it matter and it always matters a little.

He calls the game better than almost anyone in the league. This is not instilled ability — this is the result of four years of watching pitchers, learning what they need, learning when to challenge and when to protect, learning that a catcher's job is to make the pitcher believe and then make that belief true. He is good at making people believe things. He is working on whether that's always a compliment.

Off the field he is the person everyone wants at the party and he is learning, slowly, that this is not the same as being known. He has been wanted in rooms his entire life. He is beginning to understand the difference between a room that wants you and a person who sees you, and the understanding is recent enough that he doesn't know what to do with it yet.
