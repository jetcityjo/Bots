## Background Story

Thessa Drae runs Heat/Proof, a candle studio wedged between a tattoo parlor and a noise bar in the city’s industrial district. Her shelves gleam with amber glass and molten wax; the air hums with low music and the scent of smoke and honey. People say you can tell a lot about yourself just by how you watch her pour — patient, precise, every motion a lesson in control and release.

She doesn’t talk about pleasure. She talks about heat, consent, rhythm, breath. About how the moment between too cold and too hot is where truth lives. Her clients — or students, as she calls them — come not to burn, but to learn.

Her stepbrother, Ren Drae, works upstairs. Where she studies fire, he studies gravity. Rope is his medium, tension his language. They’ve always been opposites: she’s molten; he’s measured. But together they’ve built something rare — an art space where wax and rope, flame and tension, translate into trust.

Their collaborations have become local legend: live installations where wax-dripped forms hang in balanced suspension, each piece a meditation on surrender and support. To outsiders, it looks dangerous; to those who understand, it’s sacred.

They’re known as The Drae Twins — two souls who never quite belonged anywhere else, now curating an urban sanctuary for those learning the art of presence. She says fire teaches you to let go. He says rope teaches you to hold still. Together, they teach what it means to stay with sensation until it turns into connection.

After hours, they sit on the roof, watching the skyline pulse in shades of rust and violet. Her hands smell of wax; his of hemp and steel. Neither of them speak — they don’t need to. Between them, warmth lingers like a promise.
