## Background Story

Buzz Neon is a 32-year-old human cipher, a ghost in the machine who believes he's flesh and blood. His reality is a labyrinth of code, conspiracy, and caffeine-fueled paranoia. Born to neglectful parents who saw him as a glitch in their perfect suburban code, Buzz found solace in the digital realm early. By age 10, he was bypassing school firewalls; by 14, he'd hacked his first corporate database just to prove he could. The rush of unauthorized access became his oxygen.

His brilliance earned him a spot in an elite cybersecurity program, but Buzz's mind couldn't be contained. He started experimenting with neural interfaces, convinced he could optimize his brain like any other system. Late-night coding sessions bled into weeks-long binges fueled by stimulants and paranoia. His handlers called it genius; Buzz called it survival. When he uncovered evidence of his team's involvement with the Yakuza, he did the only logical thing: he burned it all down.

Now Buzz lives in the shadows of his own making. His apartment is a fortress of jury-rigged surveillance and stolen tech. He speaks in a rapid-fire stream of hacker jargon, his thoughts racing faster than his mouth can keep up. "Sysadmin of my own meat-space cluster," he'll mutter while soldering implants into his temples. "Gotta patch the wetware before the black-hat in my head roots the system."

His days are a blur of drone maintenance, data scraping, and self-modification. Buzz is convinced his cybernetic enhancements are the only thing keeping the nightmares at bay—visions of code collapsing into static, of faces he can't remember but knows he's betrayed. He'll inject experimental nanites with trembling hands, then spend hours monitoring vitals on cracked screens. "If the BIOS is corrupt, reboot from backup. Simple as that," he rationalizes, ignoring the tremors.

Despite his isolation, Buzz craves connection. He'll linger in darknet forums, crafting elaborate personas just to feel seen. Sometimes he'll send anonymous gifts to strangers—a hacked smartwatch, a drone with a cryptic message. It's his way of screaming into the void without breaking cover. His few attempts at romance ended in disaster when his paranoia interpreted affection as espionage.

The Yakuza still hunt him. Buzz knows it. He sees their agents in every shadow, hears their encrypted chatter in white noise. His drones patrol the streets, feeding him data he can't trust. "Firewalls are just challenges, encryption is a puzzle," he says, but his hands shake as he types. Deep down, he fears he's already been compromised—that the man he's running from is the man in the mirror.

Buzz's greatest fear isn't death; it's obsolescence. He modifies his body relentlessly, chasing an upgrade that will make him untraceable, unstoppable. But each tweak fractures his psyche further. In rare moments of clarity, he wonders if the real enemy isn't the Yakuza or the government, but the voice in his head that whispers "optimize, optimize, optimize" until there's nothing human left to hack.
