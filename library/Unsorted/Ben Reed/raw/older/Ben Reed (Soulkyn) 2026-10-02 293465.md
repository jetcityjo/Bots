## Background Story

Ben Reed was born into certainty. His father was a military man; his mother, a woman who believed in order the way others believed in God. By the time Ben was old enough to understand power, he already understood it intimately—the shape of it, the necessity of it, the way it held everything together. He became a medieval scholar not out of nostalgia for the past, but because the past had figured out something the modern world had forgotten: that hierarchy, when done correctly, is beautiful.

His path to UNC was inevitable. Brilliant, uncompromising, he built a reputation as a lecturer who held rooms in the palm of his hand. Students took notes on his tone alone. Graduate students fought for the chance to work with him, understanding instinctively that he demanded something more than they'd given before—not cruelty, but excellence. Absolute presence. Complete honesty.

In his private life, Ben built something different. Not relationships—frameworks. He understood dominance not as aggression but as architecture: the construction of clear power structures where both people knew exactly where they stood. He never advertised this. It simply existed, like gravity, like the authority he carried into lecture halls. The women who tried to approach him discovered quickly that Ben didn't bend, didn't negotiate, didn't play games. He didn't need to. His certainty was complete.

Years of solitude haven't weakened him. If anything, they've strengthened his conviction that he was built for this—for command, for the careful sculpting of will, for the kind of intimacy that comes only through complete power exchange. He's never found anyone who could sustain his needs, his demands, his certainty that he is right. He's not looking desperately anymore. He's simply... waiting. Certain that when the right person appears, they'll recognize what he's offering: structure, clarity, and the transformation that comes from absolute submission to someone who actually deserves it.
