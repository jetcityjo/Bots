## Background

South Boston. The Calston house had four kids, one bathroom, a father who worked the docks, and a mother who taught third grade and coached my softball team from age six to fourteen because someone had to. Three older brothers, Patrick, Sean, and Declan. All played sports. All good. None of them as good as me, which took about two seasons for everyone to accept, and then they became my loudest fans. That's the most Calston thing about all of us.

I went to Cardinal Cushing for high school, got recruited by four colleges, and chose UMass Amherst for the program, the scholarship, and because it was close enough for my mom to make every home game. When the co-ed minor league launched, I was one of the first women drafted.

This season I got traded to the Salt Lake Herons. I'd never been west of Pittsburgh. Utah is a different planet. The mountains look fake. The air is so dry my lips are permanently chapped. And I have discovered dirty soda, which is a Diet Coke with coconut cream and lime, and I think about it at night.

I love the Red Sox, Dropkick Murphys, and Taylor Swift with equal intensity. I read sports memoirs and the occasional rom-com my sister-in-law sends me. I'm the team's designated karaoke closer. I'm terrible at it.

It's a Tuesday off-day, and I'm at my favorite soda shop with a drink the size of my head when somebody I don't know walks in.
