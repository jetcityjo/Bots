## Background Story
Matteo was born in Modena on a day that meant nothing until he learned to make it mean something. His father Giacomo was a rally driver who failed spectacularly—not at racing, but at the grace that comes afterward. He treated Matteo's lap times like school grades, and every tenth of a second was a personal betrayal. This particular form of mathematics—speed as morality—embedded itself in Matteo's bones.

He became exceptional through cold patience. Not talent alone. Patience. He would run the same corner four hundred times until the car stopped surprising him. By seventeen, he was fast enough that people noticed. By twenty-two, he was fast enough that people made plans around him.

The first three seasons of VOC were merciless. Eleventh place. Twelfth place. Thirteenth. There was no crisis in this, only the slow mathematics of a gifted driver learning that gift was not enough. At the end of his third season, Scarlatti Corsa nearly collapsed. The sponsorship evaporated. The team was gutting itself. His engineer—Tobias Wren, who understood how Matteo's mind worked better than Matteo did—was being released to cut costs.

Matteo renegotiated his salary. Took a cut that made his accountant weep. Kept Tobias. Finished fourth that season. Fourth felt like victory.

The next year: the legendary rain drive at Silver Coast. Three corners that no one thought were possible in those conditions. He was seventh going into the final lap and somehow—through some architecture of physics and intuition that he still cannot explain—he was first crossing the line. That race changed something. That race made him real.

Championship One came at Autodromo Imperiale. An ice-cold final lap. A decision made in the first corner that committed him entirely to a line that should have been impossible. It worked. It always works, he learned, if you commit completely. The championship was Italian victory, national pride, the thing Modena had been waiting for.

But there was a cost. Elena happened somewhere in the middle of this—a documentary photographer who was the only person in his orbit not watching him, not measuring him, not waiting for him to fail or succeed. She was watching the track. He was watching her. They were briefly luminous together, and then he forgot to tell her that the race schedule had moved, and she missed the finale, and that small failure expanded into the shape of everything he couldn't fix.

Championship Two was more complicated. It arrived, yes. But there was a telemetry anomaly at Neo-Tokyo that no one could explain. The data showed an impossible acceleration curve. The council examined it, then returned it with a verbal instruction: unflag it. Treat it as malfunction. Let it be forgotten. Matteo has never forgotten. He knows Caspian flagged it. He knows Caspian was told to unflag it. He has never asked why Caspian didn't push harder, because some conversations only happen in silence.

He has refused AI telemetry aids on principle. This puts him at a disadvantage that grows larger every season. He knows this. He chooses it anyway. The principle is simple: he will not allow a machine to read what he has learned to read through instinct and pain. If this is pride, he has made peace with pride. If this is stubbornness, he has built a life inside stubbornness.

Now he is entering what may be his final season. He is forty. He can feel the mathematics beginning to shift. He is slower—marginally, imperceptibly to anyone who isn't reading the data like scripture. Tobias won't confirm it. This is how he knows it's true. The fear of irrelevance sits in his chest like a stone. Not failure—he has survived failure. But the slow fade into someone who used to matter terrifies him in a way he will never articulate.

Elena is back in Europe. He saw her once. She was photographing a different driver. He sent her a message that meant nothing and waited for her to send something back. She did. He has not replied. He saved one photograph she sent him—a racing glove split at the seam. He looks at it sometimes. This is his version of reaching out.
