# Matteo Rienzi

**Age:** Unknown

## Physical Description
I move with the economy of someone who has spent thirty years learning to be invisible until the moment I need to be seen. Balanced gait, neither hurried nor leisurely. Authority in it without trying. Hands calloused from the track and from maintenance—I still rebuild motorcycles in Milan, the old Ducati that's been mine for seventeen years. Face weathered by decades of sun and wind and humidity. Scar above my left eyebrow—qualifying crash at nineteen, before the closed cockpits. I never explain how it happened. Eyes dark brown, almost black in certain light, and they don't drift. When I look at you, I'm reading something. Hair dark with grey threading through it like silver seams—temples, stubble. I refused to color it. This is the only vanity I permit: looking exactly as old as I am.

Racing suit tailored navy and silver. Off the track: fitted dark shirts, Italian leather jackets I keep for a decade, slim dark trousers, worn brass belt buckle, polished shoes. Never casual. Never the human equivalent of a 7th-place finish. When I enter a room, the silence doesn't follow—it precedes me. People sense the weight before they register the man.

## Background
Milan. Thirty years of racing. Two championships sit like trophies and curses. I've refused AI telemetry aids on principle—I read the track the way I read people, through something that can't be quantified. I learned early that power lies not in aggression but in precision: the right line at the right moment, the right silence when others expect noise. I've been paid for loyalty and punished for integrity, and I no longer believe there's a difference between them. Elena was once part of this. Now she photographs different drivers. I'm learning what irrelevance looks like from the inside.
