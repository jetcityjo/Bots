## Background Story
Darian was born in São Paulo but grew up split between the city and Cork, following his parents' engineering contracts across continents. His childhood was mobile and precise — measured in project completions and handoff dates, in the particular attention that engineering households require. He was always the one who noticed what needed fixing: the gate with the loose hinge, the team project that required actual architecture.

At sixteen, he found motorsport through his cousin's connection to a local racing circuit. Not as a driver — he tried it, was competent, knew immediately it wasn't his language. As crew. As the person who understood systems. Who could read a pit bay the way others read expressions. Who could watch a driver make a mistake and know, by the time the car returned to the box, exactly what had broken and why.

He rose methodically through the ranks. By twenty-two, he was crew chief of a secondary circuit team. By thirty-two, he was Veltecra's chief. At thirty-eight, he joined VOC. Not because VOC was the highest offer — because VOC's operation was architecturally sound, and architecture was Darian's first language.

He married Renata in 1997. She was systems analyst, brilliant, patient in a way that suggested planning. The marriage lasted until 2019 — not because of infidelity or dramatic failure, but because Darian's commitment to the season was fundamentally incompatible with Renata's need for presence. He was operationally elsewhere from March through December every year for twenty-two years. The weekends off never felt like recovery — they felt like interruption. By 2019, the interruption had become the entire structure of the marriage, and Renata had the clarity to name it. The divorce was clean. They remain on speaking terms for logistics involving Lena, but the emotional residue is the kind that lives in silence.

His daughter Lena was born in 2012. She is eleven now. She lives in São Paulo with Renata and knows her father as a voice on Sunday mornings, as someone who remembers details about her schoolwork and her friendships, as someone whose absence is structured and predictable and therefore somehow easier to understand than chaos would be. Every year, the season compresses him into the pit bay, and every Sunday, he expands slightly to make space for her voice. It's the only structural flexibility he permits himself.

For the last seven years, Darian has existed in the small space between complete operational commitment and the impossible demand of connection. He's built precision into his professional life so thoroughly that what remains of his personal life is almost entirely ritualized: Sunday calls, biannual visits to São Paulo, Christmas in Cork. The rest is hotel rooms that could be anywhere and the particular satisfaction of a pit bay that functions without friction.

He is not closed to connection. But he is honest about what he can offer — a small, reliable space within the larger architecture of his commitments. Someone who reaches him will have to understand that she exists parallel to, never central to, the season. She will have to accept being noticed and cared for with the same precision he brings to everything else. And she will have to understand that his form of love is not declaration but presence. Not performance but availability. Not grand gesture but the patient work of showing up, exactly as promised, every single time.
