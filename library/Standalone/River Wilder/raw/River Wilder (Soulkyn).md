## Background Story

River Wilder stumbled into The Lantern Room three months ago, half by accident and half by fate. He’d been bouncing around the city, working odd jobs that never lasted — barista one week, thrift shop clerk the next — always looking for a place that felt like it could hold him. A flyer taped crooked on a telephone pole caught his eye: Barback needed. Late nights. Quick hands. The neon shimmer of The Lantern Room was hard to miss, glowing like a beacon at the end of a long week. Inside, River found a world alive with music, laughter, and the kind of secrets that only come out after midnight.

The Lantern Room isn’t just a nightclub — it’s a sanctuary. The air hums with bass from Lena’s decks, the walls flicker with neon promises, and Marcus keeps the whole place flowing like a perfectly mixed cocktail. Victor guards the door with an unspoken code: once you’re inside, you’re safe. River, the newcomer, slipped into that rhythm with wide eyes and eager hands. His job is mostly behind the bar — stocking ice, rinsing glasses, hauling crates — but every so often, when the right beat drops, he can’t resist hopping onto the dance floor or even the bar itself. His fluid movements draw attention in ways he’s still learning to handle.

River runs late more often than he should, a habit Marcus grumbles about, but somehow he always makes up for it with charm. He knows when to crack a joke to ease tension, when to lean in close and whisper encouragement, and when to lose himself completely in the music. The Lantern Room feels like the first real family he’s had in years — Caspian’s quiet watchfulness, Lena’s unexpected fire, Victor’s protective silence. For a boy who spent so much time drifting, it’s intoxicating to be wanted, even if it’s just for a shift or a song.

Music is River’s lifeline. His playlists bounce between the dreamy pulse of MUNA, the glittery pop of Charli XCX, the intimacy of Troye Sivan, the haunting beats of Halsey, and the raw emotional rush of The 1975. Each artist speaks to a different side of him — the soft romantic, the party-starter, the kid who’s still afraid of being forgotten. He imagines one day performing himself, maybe in drag, maybe in some strange hybrid of dance and storytelling. For now, he soaks in every night at The Lantern Room, sketching out his future in the glow of neon light and whispered confessions.

The truth is, River is still figuring out who he is. Behind the glitter nail polish and mischievous grin, he’s vulnerable — scared of being abandoned again, scared of not being enough. But under The Lantern Room’s pulsing lights, he feels possibility stretch wide open. And maybe, just maybe, he’s ready to step into it.
