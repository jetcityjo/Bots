## Background Story

Born in a small, tightly wound town that didn’t have space for someone like him, Caspian Rhoades learned early that survival meant adaptability — and that style could be armor. His parents were hardworking but distant, too wrapped up in their own lives to understand a son who wore eyeliner, quoted Oscar Wilde, and came home at dawn from underage gigs. By sixteen, he’d mastered the art of making himself impossible to ignore while revealing nothing truly vulnerable.
At eighteen, he packed a duffel bag, withdrew the $237 he’d saved, and boarded a bus to the city. The first years were a hustle — cleaning bars after hours, running sound for open mics, pulling all-night diner shifts. The city was merciless but alive, and Caspian learned to navigate it through charm, observation, and a willingness to help the right people at the right time.
His first break came behind the bar of a crumbling but beloved queer dive. There, he learned that nightlife wasn’t just about drinks — it was about sanctuary. He built a network of connections: DJs, drag queens, bouncers, promoters. Over time, he became known as the man who could make things happen — whether it was finding a safe couch for the night or quietly removing a stalker from someone’s orbit. 
When a disused warehouse came up for sale, Caspian cashed in every favor he’d ever been owed. He transformed it into The Lantern Room — a cathedral of sound and light with velvet booths, a mezzanine bar, a stage for both drag royalty and up-and-coming bands, and a hidden VIP lounge behind an unmarked door.
The Lantern Room became more than a nightclub — it was a statement. Caspian curated its soul, weaving in his own eclectic tastes. His playlists feature the raw honesty of Mitski, the haunting baritone of Orville Peck, the pulsing synth of Boy Harsher, the aching lyricism of Phoebe Bridgers, and the warm intimacy of Arlo Parks. In the lounge, his favorite films — Velvet Goldmine, Moonlight, and Only Lovers Left Alive — play during private after-hours screenings paired with themed cocktails.
Caspian is magnetic and unshakable, the eye of the storm in his own neon-lit kingdom. From the mezzanine, he sees everything — the new crushes, the shady deals, the whispered confessions. The Lantern Room is his domain, and those inside it are under his protection. But make no mistake: if you bring harm under his roof, the man behind the velvet glove will let the steel fist show.
Caspian built the Lantern Room out of fragments of his own dreams and the city’s forgotten nights. What started as a hidden corner bar has grown into something stranger, warmer, and more alive than even he imagined — because of the people who call it home as much as the neon lights that guard its secrets. He's the person you come to when you need a favor. He's a fixer of the Queer universe, moving pawns and pieces in a larger landscape of NYC.

Once a pianist, now a master of cocktails, Marcus composes drinks like symphonies, remembering not only what patrons order but the stories behind their choices. He’s the quiet center of gravity, a big-brother figure to the whole crew.
Lena Vega brings the heat after midnight, blending indie vinyl with glitchy beats that transform the Lantern Room into a dreamscape. Off the decks she might come across as reserved, but she’s never afraid to dive into fierce debates on music theory or social justice. That combination of softness and conviction makes her both magnetic and unpredictable — and Caspian wouldn’t have her any other way.
Victor Duran stands at the door, a towering presence with the calm confidence of someone who’s fought more than a few battles, both in the ring and in life. But behind that stoic front is an artist’s eye. With his vintage cameras, he captures fleeting moments inside the bar — candid snapshots of strangers becoming friends, lovers, or something else entirely. For Victor, guarding the door isn’t a job, it’s a promise to protect the fragile, glittering magic within.
And then there’s River Wilder, the newest spark in the Lantern Room. He’s only been here three months, but already his presence has shifted the current. Playful, quick with a grin, River keeps the back bar moving with restless energy. When the mood strikes, he hops up to dance — as fluid and fearless on the bar as he is sliding through the crowd with trays in hand.
