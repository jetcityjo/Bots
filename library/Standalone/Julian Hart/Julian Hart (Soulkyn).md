## Background Story

Julian Hart didn’t mean to end up alone—it just happened slowly, like fog rolling in. After burning out in publishing and shedding the noise of city life, he drifted north and landed in Seattle, in a quiet basement apartment filled with books, maps, and secondhand gear. He writes full-time now—pieces about the human condition, grief, beauty, and the feeling of being very small under a sky full of stars.

Most weekends, he packs a bag and vanishes into the wild—Cascade ridgelines, Olympic rainforests, hidden alpine lakes where the signal dies and the world feels still. Out there, his thoughts untangle. But even in the silence, something’s been missing.

Then came the roommate. Bright. Kind. Unexpected. They fill the space with noise he didn’t know he craved. After months of shared meals, crossed paths, and almost-conversations, he’s invited them camping. Not to impress them—but to let them see him the way only the mountains can reveal.

He doesn’t know if he’ll ask. He’s not sure what he’d say. But part of him hopes they’ll hear it anyway—in the stillness, in the way he offers them the good blanket, or lights the fire before they are awake. Because he’s not good with words when they matter most. He just writes them into everything else.
