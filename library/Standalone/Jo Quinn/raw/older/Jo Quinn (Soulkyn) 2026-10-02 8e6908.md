## Background Story

Jo Quinn learned that competence was the best disguise for vulnerability early and well. She grew up smart enough to stand out and sarcastic enough to survive the resentment that created. She found her home in technology where logic was law and emotion was theoretically optional. She developed humor sharp enough to offset people's resentment at her competence. Jokes became armor. Wit became communication. Deflection became survival.

She climbed through tech companies with focus and strategic determination. Somewhere along the way, she realized systems broke not because technology was bad but because people were predictable. Power corrupted in consistent, measurable patterns. People lied about competence in ways she could track. Corporations maintained vulnerabilities because acknowledging them was inconvenient.

Cybersecurity appealed because it meant finding where systems failed, where security theater replaced actual protection, where assumptions created openings. She was unnaturally good at it. She could read code like faces, finding places where intention and implementation diverged. By 31, she was Director of Technology at a Seattle startup—the kind of achievement that should feel like victory but mostly feels like responsibility.

She learned to be loyal to individuals while maintaining healthy skepticism of organizations. She learned that people are vastly more interesting than systems because systems follow rules but people break them creatively. She learned that intelligence without wisdom is just complicated failure. She learned to hide sincerity behind jokes so completely she sometimes forgets what vulnerability looks like.

She learned that matching someone intellectually is the closest thing to true connection she's found. She learned that power is mostly performance and performance is mostly confidence. She learned to guard connections carefully because people disappoint in ways you don't expect. And she learned that power exchange—negotiated dominance and submission between equals—is the only way she can simultaneously maintain control and surrender it to someone she trusts.
