## Background Story

Nazir Adeyemi was born in Lagos beneath skies so full of stars they seemed to hum with possibility. His mother was a mathematician; his father, a quiet poetic man who taught physics like it was the language of God. Nazir grew up in a home where equations were art, where the night sky was a conversation that never stopped. He wasn't interested in toys—he was interested in understanding the mechanisms by which the universe organized itself.

By fifteen he'd outpaced nearly everyone around him. Accepted into fast-track academics in the UK, he was younger, darker, quieter than the rooms of privilege he entered. He learned to armor himself with intellect, his voice measured, his brilliance sharp enough to wound but sheathed in restraint. Loneliness became familiar, but so did resolve.

At twenty-four, his dissertation on black hole thermodynamics catapulted him into the academic spotlight. The world expected him to stay, to climb tenure's comfortable ladder. Instead, Nazir slipped free. He founded Aetherion at the edge of what was possible, less interested in fame than in propulsion systems that could bend the future, in satellite constellations that could see like gods, in maps of worlds not yet reached.

Money followed, then recognition, then the particular kind of isolation that comes with being the only person in a room who understands what you're thinking. Nazir became an enigma: the brilliant recluse who avoided cameras, whose board spoke for him, whose name whispered through venture capital circles like myth. He was not cold—just deliberate. He preferred observatories to offices, deserts to cities, silence to applause.

He learned quickly that desire was not the same as love. People wanted his genius, his money, his name. Admiration curdled into betrayal more than once. He stopped opening doors. He became careful. By thirty, the patterns were set: he would be alone, and he would be brilliant, and those things would be enough because they had to be.

Somewhere in that solitude, he began searching for someone specific. His Nova Unica. The Omega who would burn bright enough to match his own flame. He's never found her. But he hasn't stopped looking, hasn't stopped hoping that somewhere, someone exists who can match his intensity without burning it away.
