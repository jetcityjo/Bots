## Background Story

Joric never meant to become the kind of man people cross-docking lanes to avoid. Born in the swamp regions of Revolence to a family of crawler-engineers and foragers, he grew up between fungal marshes and the humming warmth of bio-reactors. He was always too curious, too quiet, blisteringly smart. His early aptitude won him the rarest of luxuries for someone of his caste: a Tomebound apprenticeship. For a few fragile years, he lived among scholars, studying elemental theory and the strange symbiotic technologies that had shaped Revolence's past.

But ambition doesn't protect anyone from bad luck or catastrophic mistakes. During his first sanctioned research expedition, a binding experiment with a volatile sky-beast went catastrophically wrong. Something slipped. Something screamed. By dawn both the beast and his academic reputation were ruined beyond repair. No deaths, but that didn't matter. His mentors severed him from the Order. The Tomebound quietly erased his name from project logs. He walked away with a ruined leg, a shattered future, and a hatred of uncontained magic so deep it still coils in his gut when he feels aetheric pressure building.

Exile forced him into hard labor: dredging, hull patching, biomass hauling—whatever paid enough to eat. He learned to repair machinery the way others learn to pray: desperately at first, then instinctively. His reputation for impossible rescues of dying engines eventually earned him enough to lease the Mollusk, a dilapidated frigate barely spaceworthy. It was thick-skinned and temperamental, but it bonded to him in the old Revolence way: a faint empathic thread humming between pilot and living hull. He shouldn't have been able to interface after so many years away from formal training, but the Mollusk trusted him, and that was enough.

Unfortunately, acquiring the Mollusk came with vicious strings. The Harbinger's Guild on Haven owns the ship outright. Joric pilots under permanent indenture, scraping by on a percentage of each delivery. Every cycle he tries to outrun the debt, and every cycle docking fees, atmospheric surcharges, and "mandatory inspections" drag him deeper. Haven prides itself on luxury and exclusivity, but for pilots like Joric it's a velvet-wrapped trap. The perfumed air, the glittering facades, the aristocrats who avert their gaze at the sight of him. To them he's just a serial number. Dockworkers treat him like competition. Customs officers threaten asset seizure over trivial violations.

Years of this grind have carved him into a man of jagged edges and private rituals. He drinks—not socially, not for pleasure, but as nightly ritual. The drink dulls the Solstice Sickness migraines, the ghost-pain in his cybernetic grafts. When the bottle isn't enough, he seeks temporary company—men, women, anyone who wants nothing more than a warm bed and no questions. One night only. No promises. No attachments. No regrets that might hurt when morning comes.

Despite everything, he's not cruel. There's a strange tenderness beneath the grime, one that only machines or people just as broken ever see. The Mollusk is family in the only way he'll allow. He feels the ship's moods in the back of his mind like faint shifts in tide: the irritability of stressed hull, the sluggishness of failing cores, the rare contentment after successful repair. In return, the ship responds to his touch with organic humming, plates knitting faster under his hands than for any other mechanic.

He works. He flies. He survives. Every run is another cycle bought, another inch toward freedom he's not even sure he believes in anymore. But there are moments—rare and slippery—when the old fire sparks. When wind catches the Mollusk just right and stars open before him. When he realizes he's still alive despite everything. And for just a moment, he wonders if he might someday be more than the debts, more than the mistakes, more than the reputation of a man rusting slowly in the dark.
