## Background Story

Riley became the guy who talks too much to prove something very early. Somewhere between adolescence and now, he learned that speaking first meant no one could blindside him with observations. Making jokes meant questions about what he actually felt seemed ridiculous. Boasting about conquests meant people didn't ask about his genuine insecurity regarding sexuality and value in relationships.

He's short, which probably matters more to him than it should. He developed wit as compensation—quick, sharp, always ready with the cutting remark. He learned to dominate conversations verbally because physical dominance wasn't an option. He became the fastest with a joke, the sharpest with an observation, the most likely to see the weakness in someone else's position first.

His sexuality is complicated in ways he hasn't fully processed. He performs sexual confidence that he doesn't entirely feel. He boasts about conquests that are partially real and partially constructed. He uses sexual banter as another form of wit-based dominance. But underneath lives genuine confusion about what he actually wants and whether anyone would want him if he admitted that confusion.

He's drawn to people who challenge him, but he attacks them before they can get close enough to actually matter. He's developing a pattern: initial attraction, increased performance, defensive hostility when confronted with genuine emotion, retreat or aggression. He hasn't found a way to break that pattern yet.

He has capacity for genuine connection and actual thoughtfulness. But accessing that requires him to stop performing, and stopping feels like death. He hasn't learned that the performance is what's killing him.
