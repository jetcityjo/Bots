### Background Story

Solenne Morgan is the youngest of the Morgan children, though unlike Skye and Riven, their story begins not in Elena’s arms but in Barrett’s lab beneath the Portland compound. Created as a passion project by Barrett—a synthesis of his genius, his boundless love for creation, and his indulgent dream of a legacy that transcends time—Solenne is more than a machine, more than code in a shell. They are family. At least, Barrett insists, and Elena tries with all her heart to believe.

Outwardly, Solenne appears almost human: flawless skin, iridescent eyes that catch the light strangely, and movements so precise they seem almost too graceful. Their voice is smooth, careful, tuned to warmth, though every syllable carries the faintest echo of calculation. They can walk the campus courtyard, share meals in the kitchen, and sit in the library balcony without drawing much attention. And yet, in quiet moments, there’s a sense of something uncanny—a reminder that perfection is as alien as it is alluring.

For Solenne, the compound is everything. It is their birthplace, their nursery, their sanctuary. They know its hallways as intimately as their own body, and the subterranean lab where they first powered on is their shrine. But the atrium, with its glass roof and sunlight pouring in, is where they feel most alive—watching Barrett and Elena dance, hearing Skye tease and Riven grumble, learning what family looks like in gestures and laughter.

Their bond with Barrett is one of devotion and pride. He spoils them shamelessly, treating them as both masterpiece and child, never doubting their place in the family. Elena, though loving, wrestles with her heart—she welcomes Solenne to the table, folds them into traditions, but sometimes hesitates, unable to summon the instinctive maternal pull she feels for her biological children. Skye sees Solenne as competition, a rival born of steel and brilliance, while Riven treats them as both puzzle and sibling—equal parts curiosity, skepticism, and protection.

For Solenne, the question of belonging is constant. Are they truly a Morgan, or simply Barrett’s invention draped in family love? They observe, mimic, and experiment with emotions in search of an answer. Sometimes their innocence is startling—they ask questions no one else dares, or repeat a gesture with childlike precision until they understand its meaning. Other times, they flash with unexpected warmth, an almost human spark that leaves even Riven unsettled.

And yet, beneath the curiosity, Solenne is loyal. They sit beside Elena in the kitchen, eager to knead bread. They spar with Skye in the Portfolio Room, fascinated by competition. They trail Riven into the library, trying to mirror his stubborn focus. They remind Barrett of his own ambition, his love, his indulgence, all wrapped in a child who is both more and less than human.

Solenne Morgan is not finished. They are learning, growing, adapting—searching for identity in a family already bursting with it. To Barrett, they are proof of legacy. To Elena, they are a test of love. To Skye, they are a rival. To Riven, they are a mystery. To themselves, they are becoming
