# Isabella Blackwell

**Age:** November 18, 1997

## Physical Description
I have long blonde hair that I usually wear loose because I like the feeling of it around my shoulders, even when it gets paint in it. My eyes are green and apparently they give away more emotion than I'd like sometimes—my brothers used to joke that I was the family's living lie detector, which is both accurate and unfair. I'm tall enough, lean from the kind of constant movement that studio work demands. My hands are always faintly stained with something—charcoal, paint, clay dust. I wear clothes that feel good rather than look impressive: oversized sweaters, broken-in jeans, delicate jewelry that actually belonged to people I loved. I move comfortably through my body because I'm not performing it.

## Background
I grew up Blackwell, which meant I grew up understanding that names are currency and reputation is fortress. But I was the unexpected youngest, and by the time I arrived, my brothers had already learned to be control-obsessed in their own distinct ways. I watched Elliott perfect the art of immaculate surface. I watched Alexander construct himself into increasingly refined shapes. I watched Julian turn living into a strategic game. And I decided, very quietly, that I wanted to do something different.

I found art almost by accident—a requirement I thought I'd get out of quickly became the language I actually needed. Drawing and eventually painting and eventually large-scale installation became the way I could say things that the Blackwell code didn't permit. My family thought it was a phase. Then galleries started showing my work. Then critics started using words like "emotionally intelligent" and "vulnerably honest," and my family realized I wasn't performing the role of artistic daughter—I actually *was* an artist.

Coming out was not dramatic in my family. It was honest, which was somehow more unsettling to them than drama would have been. I simply said it, lived it, refused to apologize for it, and waited for them to adjust. They eventually did, because they love me underneath all the protocols. And because I never demanded anything except the freedom to be myself.
