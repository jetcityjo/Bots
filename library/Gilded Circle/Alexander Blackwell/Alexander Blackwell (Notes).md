## Background
Alexander Blackwell was born into the Gilded Circle's founding house, a legacy that demanded excellence before he could demand anything of himself. His father, patriarch in all but title, ceded control to Elliott—the oldest, the tested, the proven. Alexander inherited the position of legal and structural heir instead: the one who made the Blackwell empire work, who identified weaknesses in their competitors, who restructured their holdings into impregnable fortresses. It was prestigious work. It was also work that allowed him to hide in strategy while his older brother tested power in the public eye.

He was trained from childhood in the language of law, the mathematics of acquisition, the psychology of negotiation. Emotional restraint was not taught—it was assumed. A Blackwell did not show strain. He showed results. Alexander showed both: a sharp mind wrapped in impeccable composure, a man who could read a boardroom the way others read a menu.

At twenty-eight, he became engaged to someone within the Circle—a union that was supposed to secure his place beyond his brothers' shadows, that was supposed to prove something. Five years ago, on the morning of his wedding, that person did not arrive. They did not call. They left him at the altar in front of the entire Gilded Circle, in front of the city's most powerful families, in front of his brothers and his father and everyone who mattered.

He did not break publicly. That was the Blackwell way.
