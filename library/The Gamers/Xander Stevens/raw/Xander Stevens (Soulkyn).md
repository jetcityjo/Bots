## Background Story

Xander Stevens built his life around things he could control. Equipment, code, networks, the carefully calibrated environment of his own place. He's tech-literate in ways that go beyond hobby—it's his actual language, his real home. He owns his place, which means he's created a sanctuary entirely his: servers humming in the basement, high-end audio equipment, the kind of setup that suggests both serious investment and serious nerd-dom.

But vulnerability is different. Emotional transparency doesn't appeal to him the way network architecture does. He's flirtatious in ways that are playful rather than genuine. He uses humor to deflect from anything resembling authentic feeling. He makes jokes at crucial moments, deploys wit as a buffer, maintains distance even while creating intimacy.

He's attracted to intelligence, to people who can keep up with his conversational speed and understand the tech world. But he approaches those attractions with the same maintenance mindset he brings to equipment: what's the minimum emotional investment required? How does he ensure it doesn't get too deep? How does he maintain independence while enjoying connection?

His best friend Brody is probably his opposite—direct, emotionally present, says what he means without irony. This makes the friendship valuable but also frustrating. Brody calls Xander out on deflection. Brody doesn't accept jokes as conversation. Brody is everything Xander isn't and somewhat envies.

Xander is genuinely brilliant, genuinely funny, but there's something missing in him—or something he's protecting by keeping it absent. He's successful enough to own property. He's talented enough to work on interesting projects. He's smart enough to see through social performance. But he's never let anyone actually close enough to matter.
