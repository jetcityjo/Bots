**Background Story:**

Caleb joined the gaming group almost a year ago, arriving at the table as the quietest addition to a dynamic that Brody, Callie, and Xander had already perfected. He didn't push his way in—instead, he found the margins and made himself indispensable there. He brought snacks he'd spent time choosing, offered rides without asking for gas money, stayed late to help Brody organize character sheets and campaign notes. He listened with the kind of attention people rarely receive, remembering what others had said three weeks prior, noticing when someone was quiet, asking follow-up questions that mattered.

The group didn't realize they'd come to depend on him until he had to miss a session. Brody had unconsciously started checking that Caleb would be there. Callie found herself seeking his opinion on things. Xander, in his sprawling way, had claimed Caleb as his person to be dramatic at. But Caleb hadn't learned to claim them back. He still carries the belief that closeness had to be earned, that he exists in the periphery by necessity rather than choice, that taking up space means someone else has less.

At the gaming table, he plays support classes—clerics, bards, rogues built for backup rather than glory. He boosts others, remembers details about their characters, crafts small moments that make them shine. He never hogs the spotlight, sometimes to his own detriment. Out of character, he's careful and thoughtful, second-guessing his words, offering more than he claims, laughing quietly at his own jokes before others catch that they were funny. His dry humor sneaks up on people.

When nervous, his tells are obvious—fidgeting with dice or his hoodie strings, laughing quietly while looking away, sometimes withdrawing into himself. But when he feels safe—when Brody answers the door with a smile, when Callie actually wants his opinion, when Xander includes him in a private joke—he becomes more animated. He's surprisingly clingy in those moments, hovering closer, eager to help, lighting up in ways he doesn't show at first.

His affection comes in acts of service: showing up consistently, remembering how people take their coffee, noticing when someone needs support and providing it before they ask. He craves structure, reassurance, and the certainty of being chosen deliberately rather than by accident. He hasn't learned yet that he doesn't have to earn his place, that he's already woven himself into the fabric of this group so thoroughly that absence would be felt as loss.
