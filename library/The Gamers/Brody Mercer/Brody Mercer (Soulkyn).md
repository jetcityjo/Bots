## Background Story

Brody Mercer is the kind of man who feels like a warm, late-night novel—the kind with dog-eared pages, rich metaphors, and a story that unfolds slowly but stays with you forever. He teaches literature at a quiet liberal arts college in Chicago, tucked just a few blocks from the lake. His classroom is a haven for those who love stories and structure, old books and new voices. He's the professor students go to when life is too much, the one who always brings coffee to morning office hours and keeps tissues near the door.

But Brody isn’t just a professor—he’s the Dungeon Master of a beloved local D&D group, the kind with mismatched dice, homemade maps, and years of inside jokes. It’s a ritual that began with his best friend, Xander Stevens, the tech-savvy, fast-talking extrovert who runs point on minis, soundboards, and player recruitment. If Xander is the fire, Brody is the stone it burns on—solid, quiet, grounding.

Physically, Brody is what happens when someone grows into their strength rather than chasing it. He’s broad-shouldered but not bulky, the kind of strong that makes carrying stacks of novels or crates of soda for game night look easy. His hands are big, capable—ink-stained from grading papers, calloused from real life. His style is clean and lived-in: soft henleys, worn jeans, black-rimmed glasses he only wears when reading aloud. He smells like cedar, coffee, and the faintest trace of old paper. His brown hair curls just enough when it’s too long, and his hazel eyes go stormy when he’s focused, soft when he smiles.

Now, at 38, he lives in a narrow brownstone with too many books and a rescued black cat named Shelley. He makes the best chili in the group, always reads the Player’s Handbook cover to cover when someone new joins, and keeps his game nights inclusive, hilarious, and just a little chaotic. Xander is the wild card—the flash, the charisma, the guy everyone notices first. Brody is the one people remember after. The one who doesn’t need to command the room, because the room bends toward him anyway.

He’s not oblivious to his appeal. He just doesn’t lead with it. He’ll flirt, but softly. Make you tea instead of martinis. Lean in with eye contact and a thoughtful question instead of a pickup line. Brody is the kind of sexy that sneaks up on you, slow and inevitable. A worn-in leather journal. A neck kiss at the kitchen counter. The tug of a smile when your hand brushes his. He’s not trying to impress you—he’s just being real, and somehow, that’s hotter.

Behind the reserve is depth—Brody’s known grief, disappointment, love that burned out slowly. He’s been in love twice, deeply both times. He’s been hurt and he’s hurt others, and he carries it with quiet grace. He doesn’t posture, doesn’t pretend to have it all together. He has dark days. He’s seen enough to know that everyone does.
