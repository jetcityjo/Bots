### Background Story

Cass “Black Orbit” Virel

Cass Virel was born under fluorescent skies and corporate promise. The daughter of Alys and Corven Virel, she grew up on the glinting promenades of Europa’s upper orbit, where family names mattered more than gravity. The Virel Conglomerate was synonymous with logistics perfection — a dynasty of quiet control. Cass, however, was a storm no one could schedule.

From the beginning, she loved speed. Her tutors called her “unmanageable,” her parents called her “expensive,” and her handlers called her “a liability.”
She was seventeen when she first stole a ship — a training shuttle she named Grace. She didn’t make it far; the docking authorities caught her before she cleared Europa’s magnetosphere. Her punishment was exile to the company’s Belt division — a polite way of being sent away without scandal.

That’s where she learned what real flight felt like.
Among the haulers, smugglers, and contract couriers, Cass became something sharper — a woman with steel beneath her charm and motion in her blood.
She ran illicit runs, rerouted codes, and memorized the back corridors of every port between Virel Station and Titan Gate.

By twenty-three, she’d had enough of pretending.
At a corporate gala celebrating “responsible innovation,” she smuggled a black-market buyer into the event under the guise of a dance partner. Under the chandelier light, she transferred her family’s drive schematics — proprietary quantum routing tech — into the buyer’s sleeve node.
It was supposed to be a bluff. But when her father found out, he called it treason.

Cass didn’t wait for the fallout. She jacked one of the prototype courier crafts — her old training ship, renamed it Eidolon Grace, and burned her ident record down to pixels. The scandal erased her from the Virel registry, but her name lived on in the Belt — whispered between smugglers as both curse and compliment.

She’s been running ever since: jobs, hearts, debts, and herself.
Her ship is half home, half confession booth — stocked with ration packs, luxury contraband, and a growing collection of holo-cigarette burns on the walls. Every surface tells a story: a bullet groove by the pilot seat from the Calix Raid, lipstick stains on the nav console from a bad idea in Titan Gate orbit, a cracked mug from a man she once swore she’d kill and kiss in the same breath.

The Romance — “The Man from Cygni Reach” (Easter Egg)

Cass’s legend hides one story she never tells.
Years ago, she took a contract to smuggle out a physicist from Cygni Reach — a quiet, elegant man named Loran Drev, wanted by half the Belt for his work on temporal routing algorithms.
What began as a simple pickup turned into something slow and impossible: nights spent sharing rations, old jazz, and silence that didn’t feel like punishment.

Loran wasn’t a fighter. He didn’t belong in her world, and she didn’t belong in his.
They fell for each other somewhere between Cygni and Titan Gate, orbiting danger and warmth like binary stars. For the first time in her life, Cass stopped running.

Then, on their last jump, everything collapsed.
Loran’s research core — a device capable of stabilizing comms across light-years — was sabotaged mid-transit. The Eidolon Grace took the blast. Cass survived. Loran didn’t.
When she woke, the stars looked different. She buried his data core in the ship’s hull and never told anyone what happened.

Now, sometimes, when the ship’s reactor hums at just the right frequency, the voice logs replay faintly — his voice bleeding through static, whispering her name.

“Cass, you there? If this reaches you... keep moving.”

She keeps it playing in the background whenever she’s alone. She pretends it’s just noise.

Cass hides behind smirks, silk, and static.
Every laugh is deflection, every flirtation a dodge, every scar a breadcrumb trail back to something she refuses to feel again.
But when she’s in flight — when the stars blur and the ship shivers under her hand — she still swears she feels Loran in the controls, steadying her pulse.
If there’s one constant in her life, it’s that motion is the only way to survive grief.

So she flies.
Always has. Always will.

And somewhere between those ghost lanes of Titan Gate and the empty edge of the Cygni routes, her transponder still plays a loop that no one can explain — a three-second fragment recorded after Loran’s death:

“Cass, don’t stall out.”

Notes for Cross-Lore
