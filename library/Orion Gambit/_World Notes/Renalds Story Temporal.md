# Renald's Story (Temporal Version)

**Title:** *Dead Air (The Long Delay)*

**Concept:** Nova Kai and Renald are never contemporaries. Their voices crossed through a collapsed relay — a one-in-a-billion comms glitch that allowed a woman from the 28th century and a man from the 32nd to speak, just for a while.

## 1. The Listener Who Heard a Ghost
Renald worked as a Signal Archivist for the Orion Relay Museum, centuries after the age of live couriers had ended. One evening, while restoring a courier frequency from the DHA-29 archive, a ping came through the line — faint, real-time, impossible.

It was Nova Kai. Her voice came with date metadata four hundred years old.

Then she said his name.

## 2. The Impossible Channel
Renald rerouted power through a deprecated relay array near Sol's orbit. The signal refused to collapse. Every night, he tuned in, and her transmissions came through again — delayed, distorted, but alive.

**Nova:** "So where are you, exactly?"
**Renald:** "Somewhere you've already been."

## 3. The Temporal Drift
They realized slowly that their worlds didn't match. Nova spoke of Mars still half-terraformed, couriers still alive, ships still crewed by humans. Renald's time had none of that — only archives and drones, empty space and echoes.

*"You're the ghost," she said once.*
*"Maybe," he replied. "But you're the one who's already gone."*

## 4. The Convergence Window
Solar flare season opened the rift wider — the Delay Bloom phenomenon. For one stretch of 36 hours, their voices aligned perfectly.

Nova said she'd reach Sol in two days. Renald promised he'd be waiting.

*"Maybe time will catch up," he said.*
*"Maybe it already did," she whispered.*

## 5. The Collapse
When the relay finally broke — burned out by radiation and age — the connection went quiet mid-sentence. Renald kept transmitting long after it failed.

"Some messages are too old to deliver."

## 6. Centuries Later
Archaeologists found both sets of transmissions encoded deep within the same relay. Nova's logs were dated 2791. Renald's were from 3118.

When played together, the data formed a harmonic waveform pattern matching two heartbeats and a faint trumpet line.

The file is now known as **The Long Delay**.

Visitors report hearing two voices fading in and out of each other:
"You always were faster than light."
"And you… were worth the wait."
