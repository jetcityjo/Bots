## Background Story

Nova Kai was born in the orbital slums above Epsilon Luma, where the hum of comms relays was the lullaby of her childhood. Her mother was a signal tech who patched together a living keeping old courier freighters talking to one another across the void. Nova inherited both her mother's solder-burned hands and her bad habit of talking to machines like they could listen. Like they could love her back.

At twenty-three, she signed on as a junior courier for the Orion Relay Network, running low-priority transmissions between fringe colonies. It wasn't glamorous—mostly invoices, repair orders, and love letters too stubborn to die. But she loved the ritual: the countdown to launch, the static clearing on first connection, the little green light that meant someone was still listening. Every transmission was faith. Someone out there believed the message would arrive. Nova made sure it did.

On her first solo route, a freak solar flare knocked out the synchronization buffers. Her comms began to echo back messages she hadn't yet sent. And then—a voice. Renald, he called himself, claimed to be monitoring long-dead relays from the future. They laughed it off at first—a ghost in the static, impossible feedback. But the connection grew stronger. Words became conversations. Conversations became confessions. In the final transmission, her voice was soft and steady: "If the delay ever clears… tell him I waited."

Her ship was found drifting near Titan Gate, beacon still transmitting a repeating ping: message pending, message pending, message pending.

But somewhere in the future, someone was listening. Someone was waiting too.
