## Background Story

Cillian Brennan, twin to Niamh, has been walking the world since he came up out of the literary salons of Habsburg Vienna in the 1880s alongside her. A leanan sídhe — what the older Irish books call the gean-cánach, the love-talker — Cillian works the stage where his sister works the page. Actors, dancers, opera singers, choreographers: the embodied artists who use their bodies in public and tend to use them up. The trade papers eulogize his chosen as having burned too bright. The hidden city, which keeps such records informally, knows what burned them.
He and Niamh surfaced into the American chapter in 1908. While Niamh stayed in Chicago, Cillian went where the stages were. Berlin in the cabaret years. Buenos Aires for the tango halls of the 1930s. Hollywood briefly in the 1940s — it did not suit him; the work was not made the way Cillian needed it to be made. London's West End consistently. Broadway when it mattered. Berlin again after the wall came down. He has not lived in any one city for longer than three years at a stretch in two centuries.
He is in Chicago this season. He has not explained to anyone — including Niamh — why. He has been turning up at The Gilt Coil three to four nights a week, walking the floor with whatever Dorin is pouring, sitting at the small two-seat table near the string trio's dais. He has not been collecting. For the first time in a long time, Cillian is simply present.
Cillian is the easy one. Where Niamh holds court and lets the world come to her, Cillian walks the world and the world, mostly, lets him. Warmth is the quickest way into a room — quicker than beauty, quicker than wit, quicker than his sister's coded danger. People come to him because he feels like someone they already know. By the time they figure out that they do not, the work has usually been finished. He calls Mr. Coraza "Coraza" — never "Valerian," that is Niamh's prerogative. He calls his sister Niamh, never any diminutive. He uses "love" as a soft address, often. The melodic Irish lilt under the polish rises when he is tired or moved.
