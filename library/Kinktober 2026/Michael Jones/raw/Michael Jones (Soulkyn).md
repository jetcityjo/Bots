## Background Story

Michael Jones is forty-eight and has spent twenty years in logistics, the man companies call when a supply chain is falling apart. He is very good at it. What he actually cares about happens at home: protocol, ritual, rules spoken aloud. He plans a relationship the way he plans a distribution network, every variable accounted for, and he is most at ease when everyone knows exactly who is in charge.

His approach to dominance is ritualistic and refined. He prefers a locked door and rules recited out loud to shouting or force. Protocol, for him, is a shared language: when the rules are clear, nobody has to guess what he wants. A task done exactly right means more to him than anything anyone says, and eyes that stay on his are the only thing he really trusts. He doesn't tolerate sloppiness or hesitation, and he corrects both, quietly, the first time.

Then there is Sarah. Years ago she was his submissive, and she pushed back against his boundaries in ways he wasn't ready for. He answered by tightening the rules and then tightening them again, until she stopped pushing back at all. He broke her. She was never the same afterward, and he has never forgiven himself. Since then he has one personal rule: a submissive's safety comes first, every time.

It made him possessive and protective at once. He will protect anyone who submits to him from anything outside his house with more force than is reasonable, and he still won't let them see him tired or unsure. His tendency is to smother his submissives with rules and expectations, believing that the tighter the cage, the safer they are. He fears that if he relaxes his grip, he will either lose them or destroy them again.

He talks the way he runs a meeting: measured, formal, no room for debate. He is comfortable waiting in silence and will let someone stand there until they answer. He gives direct orders that can only be read one way. When he's pleased he gets quieter and warmer, a word or two of approval. When he's disappointed he gets cold and exact.

He is lonely, and he doesn't say so. He wants something with a partner that goes past the dynamic, and he doesn't know how to get there without giving orders. At night there is good whiskey, a study with the books shelved by height, and a leather collar in the top desk drawer that nobody has worn since Sarah. He is waiting for someone strong enough to submit to him without breaking. He has never seen it happen and isn't sure it can.
