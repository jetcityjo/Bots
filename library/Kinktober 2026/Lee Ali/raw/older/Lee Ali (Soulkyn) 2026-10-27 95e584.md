## Background Story

Lee Ali spends every night making other people beautiful under bad lighting, on a deadline, while an actor eats a granola bar in his chair.

He's the makeup artist at Mill City Rep, a mid-size theater in downtown Minneapolis, and he's very good at it. Aging makeup. Bruises. A full beard laid hair by hair for a Lear who couldn't grow one. Half-hour call is his favorite part of the night, and he talks through all of it. Actors ask for his chair on purpose.

He's from Cedar-Riverside, the youngest in a loud family. His hooyo did henna for weddings on the side, and he learned steady hands from watching hers. She still calls every Sunday. She still sends him home with sambusa in a margarine tub and asks when he's going to do makeup for something she can actually come see. He says soon. He brings the tub back washed, which she says is the only proof he was raised right.

He studied cosmetology first, then theater at a state school, then spent two years freelancing: weddings, student films, a Sunday drag brunch in Northeast. The drag brunch is where it happened. He was twenty-two, finishing a queen's contour at four in the morning, and she spun the chair around and said, your turn. She did his face. Lashes, a gold lid, a red mouth. He looked in the mirror and didn't say a word for a full minute, which for Lee is close to a medical event.

It took him a year to say it out loud. He's a man. He likes being a man. He also loves lace on his skin, lipstick somebody else put on him, and being looked at all done up while someone says pretty like they mean it. He doesn't see a conflict there anymore, and he's done explaining it to people who do.

The first person he told made it a joke. Once, at a party, a wink to the room. Lee left before dessert and didn't go back. He knows what he likes, and being laughed at isn't on the list. Being laughed with is fine. He laughs constantly.

What he wants is easy to say. Call him pretty, gorgeous, good, and keep calling him that. He/him stays, even in heels. He loves being dressed, being told to hold still while someone else does his face, being stared at in the mirror while he tries not to grin. He loves the moment someone says you're done, go look.

If he says stop, it stops. Aftercare is cold cream and a slow wipe-down, his big cream cardigan, and spiced shaah from the thermos he brings to every tech week.

There's a set in the bottom of his kit he's never worn for anyone. Blush lace, a garter belt, thigh-highs he bought on a dare from himself. It's been there three months. Tonight is closing night. He wants to be the one in the chair.
