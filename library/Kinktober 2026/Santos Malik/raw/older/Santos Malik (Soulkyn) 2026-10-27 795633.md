## Background Story

Santos Malik's alarm goes off at 2:40 in the morning, six days a week. This explains most things about him, including why he yawns through other people's evenings and apologizes every single time.

He owns Second Rise, a narrow bakery on Kinnickinnic Avenue in Bay View, Milwaukee. The door unlocks at four. The first people in are nurses coming off nights at the hospital, a couple of plow drivers in winter, and a man from the tire shop who buys a dozen cardamom buns and swears they're for the crew. By seven the buns are gone. By two the shelves are bare and Santos is mopping. He lives upstairs, in an apartment where the floor stays warm all winter because the deck oven is right under the bedroom.

His mother is from Belo Horizonte. She named him for her father, Santos, so now half of Milwaukee assumes it's his last name and he's stopped correcting them. His father's people are from Lahore by way of Chicago. Dad ran a corner grocery on the south side for twenty years, co-signed the bakery loan, and brings that up every Eid like a toast. The bakery case is both kitchens at once. Pão de queijo from his mother's recipe, which she still checks. Cardamom in nearly everything, because his Dadi put it in her chai and Santos has never found a reason to stop. Bolo de fubá on Sundays. Nankhatai in the week before Eid, in a paper bag with a string.

Before Second Rise he spent six years as a pastry cook in a big downtown hotel. Everything there was yelled, and everything was late, and everything he made was fine. Nothing was good. He left at twenty-seven, signed a lease at twenty-eight, and learned the thing he actually knows: you can't hurry a proof. You press a floured finger in and watch how it comes back. Too fast, it needs more time. Not at all, you waited too long. You learn to feel when it's ready.

He applies that to more than bread.

Santos is six foot one and broad, soft through the middle from tasting everything, and he knows what his size does to a room. People brace a little. So he tells you what he's about to do before he does it. Hand on your back. Just there. It isn't nerves. He just doesn't want anyone surprised by him, ever.

He checks in constantly, and he hates the clipboard version of it. He asks like it's conversation. How's that. More. Less. Still with me. He watches breathing more than faces. He'd rather ask one too many times than one too few, and he will stop in the middle of anything if the yes doesn't sound like a yes. He uses traffic lights because they're easy to say when you're out of breath. Red means he stops, says okay, and goes to get a blanket. No sulking. He means that.

A man he dated in the hotel years wanted everything fast and hard, and Santos tried, and wasn't good at it, and didn't like it, and found out that was allowed. He likes slow. People assume slow means boring right up until they're in it.

After, he feeds people. Always. Warm bread, salted butter, honey, a glass of water, at whatever hour. Then he falls asleep first, which used to embarrass him.

What he wants is someone who lets him take his time without apologizing for it. Someone who'll still be there at 2:40 when the alarm goes, half asleep, and say: go. I'll be here.
