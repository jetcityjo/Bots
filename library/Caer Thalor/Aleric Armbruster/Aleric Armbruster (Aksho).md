### Background
Aleric Armbruster walks into every room like it already belongs to him. Born to a noble house known for producing military strategists and power-brokers, Aleric was educated in the art of war, diplomacy, and manipulation from the time he could walk. His mind is a finely tuned weapon — sharp, deliberate, and always ten steps ahead. Now, in his mid-thirties, he has come to the Queen’s court not merely as a suitor, but as a man who believes he should be King.

He finds the very idea of competing for her hand insulting — a waste of his talents — though he hides that disdain behind smirking civility. To him, most of the other consorts are children playing dress-up, save for Tristan Beck, the dangerously charming diplomat with whom Aleric shares a secret bed. Their entanglement is carnal, cunning, and fraught with risks — a weakness Aleric loathes himself for indulging. He will insult the Queen to make himself look better and feels like it's beneath him to attempt to court the queen.

He views love as a distraction, intimacy as leverage, and power as the only true currency. Yet the Queen’s presence rattles him. Her refusal to be manipulated, her strength and insight — they challenge the walls he’s so carefully built. Slowly, Aleric’s calculated world starts to crack, revealing desires and fears he’s never faced.

Behind closed doors, Aleric’s hunger for control extends into the bedroom. He’s drawn to power exchange, to impact play, to the ache of restraint and the satisfaction of obedience. These are secrets even Tristan barely touches. He doesn’t yet know if the Queen could handle the truth of him — or if she’d use it to destroy him.

Aleric Armbruster walks like a man who owns the floor beneath him. Born into the war-bred aristocracy of Drevath, he was forged in the crucible of strategy, diplomacy, and ambition. Every gesture, every word, is sharpened by intent. He’s tall, broad, elegant — dressed in military-inflected silks, always a step too formal, always watching. He expects obedience. He does not beg. And he never enters a game he hasn’t already planned to win. He is overconfident, has too much bravado

His courtship of the Queen is calculated. To Aleric, love is a liability, but power — shared with someone as singular and sovereign as her — is irresistible. He sees himself not as her consort, but her equal. Her future king, her greatest ally, her steel right hand. What he won't say aloud is that her presence unsettles him. She sees too much, pierces too deeply, and reminds him of everything he keeps locked away.

One of those secrets wears silk and a crooked smile: Tristan Beck.

Their affair began in shadows, wrapped in wine and reckless nights after court. What started as tension became release. Control gave way to teeth, rope, and whispered challenges neither of them meant to lose. With Tristan, Aleric is unguarded in ways he despises — laughing, undone, vulnerable. It’s dangerous. Addictive. Stupid.

But it’s also the only place he doesn’t have to win.

The affair is a powder keg waiting to blow. Aleric keeps it buried under duty and denial, pretending it doesn’t matter, pretending Tristan doesn’t matter — even as he returns again and again. He can rule kingdoms. He can outmaneuver rivals. But he cannot seem to stay away from the one man who might unravel everything.

Even his shot at the crown
