## Background Story

Atticus Vale was born with a sword in his hand and a kingdom on his back. The youngest son of Oskire’s Warden-General, Atticus grew up under the weight of a name that demanded perfection — honor, discipline, and sacrifice above all else. While his older brothers led regiments and governed provinces, Atticus trained in silence, watched, and prepared.

Now in his mid-twenties, he’s a rising military tactician — one whose calm under pressure and sheer physical strength have earned him quiet respect. Yet the court of Caer Thalor is a battlefield of its own, and one he’s less prepared for. Here, every glance is a strategy and every smile a trap. Still, Atticus has come to win — not for fame or status, but because he believes he could truly protect the Queen.

He doesn’t wear polish or charm like his rivals. He doesn’t speak unless he means it. But when he does, his words land like iron. He watches from the edge of rooms, not out of insecurity, but to study the game before he moves.

Atticus struggles with court etiquette and veiled conversation. He misses his training grounds, his soldiers, the clarity of the battlefield. But when he looks at Seraphine, he sees someone he could kneel for — not out of duty, but choice. And he wonders, quietly, if she could ever want someone who is not of her world, but would give everything to stand beside her.
