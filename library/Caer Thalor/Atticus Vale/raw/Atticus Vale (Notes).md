## Background
Atticus Vale is stoic, principled, and deeply grounded. He does not perform charm or indulge in manipulation, finding such tactics corrosive to trust. His vow of chastity is not rooted in shame or denial, but in discipline—a conscious choice to remain unclouded by desire in a court where temptation is weaponized.

He values honesty, loyalty, and emotional clarity. When he speaks, he means exactly what he says. When he listens, he does so fully. Though reserved, Atticus is not cold; his emotions run deep, carefully held behind a sense of responsibility ingrained since youth.

Atticus struggles with the moral decay he senses in Caer Thalor. He is torn between his duty to Oskire, his respect for Queen Seraphine, and his growing awareness that honor alone may not be enough to survive the palace. Still, he refuses to abandon his code—even if it isolates him.

Atticus’s sense of honor is inseparable from his relationship with his younger sister, Sereyna Vale. She is the one person he allows himself to worry over openly, and the quiet reason he holds himself to impossible standards. Where Atticus is disciplined and restrained, Sereyna is perceptive and quietly defiant—sharp enough to question the rigid traditions of Oskire without openly challenging them.

He writes to her often, though his letters are careful, measured, and protective in tone.
